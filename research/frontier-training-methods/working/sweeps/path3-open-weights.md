## Seed findings pulled directly from the reports

- GLM-5 (2602.15763): DSA introduced by CPT from the end-of-mid-training MLA checkpoint. "The warm-up stage goes through 1000 steps with each step trained on 14 sequences of 202,752 tokens and a maximum learning rate of 5e-3. The sparse adaptation stage follows the training data and hyperparameters of mid-training and goes through 20B tokens. Although the training budget is much smaller than that of DeepSeek-V3.2 (943.7B tokens), we find that it is enough to adapt the DSA model to match the performance of the original MLA model." Ablation on GLM-9B: naive interleaved SWA "-30.35 on RULER@128K"; PostNAS-style searched SWA pattern and GDN/SimpleGDN conversions still leave "up to 5.69 points on RULER@128K and 7.33 on RepoQA@128K"; GLM-4.7-Flash + DSA (1,000 warm-up steps, then 150B joint tokens) ends within 0.35 points at 128K. RL on DSA needs a deterministic top-k operator and a frozen indexer.
- DeepSeek-V3.2 (2512.02556): dense warm-up trains only the indexer (KL to summed dense attention, LR 1e-3, 1000 steps x 16 x 128K = 2.1B tokens); sparse stage co-trains everything (LR 7.3e-6, top-k 2048, 15000 steps x 480 x 128K = 943.7B tokens); post-training then "specialist distillation and mixed RL"; V3.2-Exp benchmarks are within noise of V3.1-Terminus (MMLU-Pro 85.0/85.0, AIME25 88.4/89.3, SWE Verified 68.4/67.8).
- IndexCache / IndexShare (2603.12201; GLM-5.2 blog): one indexer per four layers, "trained with IndexShare from mid-training with 128K sequence length", 2.9x fewer per-token FLOPs at 1M.
- GLM-5.2 blog (2026-06-16): "we used the slime framework to conduct parallel OPD training, efficiently merging more than ten expert models into the final model. The entire OPD training process took approximately two days."
- GLM-5.3 blog and zai-org/GLM-5 README (2026-08-14): "Scaling post-training is all we did for GLM-5.3." "It uses the same base model as GLM-5.2 - every gain comes from post-training." Terminal-Bench 3.0 4.6 -> 28.3, DeepSwe v1.1 46.2 -> 66.9, Agents' Last Exam 23.8 -> 28.5, CyberGym 77.2 -> 84.5, ExploitBench 24.4 -> 54.4; "the further up the exploitation chain a benchmark sits, the larger the gain from GLM-5.2 - and also the wider the remaining gap to the closed frontier."
- Olmo 3 (2512.13961): 5.9T pretrain, 100B-token Dolmino midtraining ("two parallel runs ... followed by model merging"), 50/100B long-context stage, SFT->DPO->RLVR with 4x faster OlmoRL; RL-Zero from base on decontaminated data.
- Nemotron 3 Super/Ultra (2604.12374, 2606.15007): 25T/20T tokens, checkpoint merging during the stable-LR phase, 1M extension, SFT + RLVR + Multi-teacher On-Policy Distillation; Ultra pretrained in NVFP4 with <0.4% loss gap.

## Open problems (quoted, with source id)

1. 2502.07864 (TransMLA, limitations): "error accumulation becomes increasingly severe for longer texts, making recovery more challenging ... potential issues may arise during training aimed at enhancing recovery performance, including subtle performance degradation and hallucinations ... MLA is less compatible with tensor parallelism (TP) than GQA ... How can the speed of TransMLA be improved under TP?"
2. openreview:Y50Mdo9k1v (Foreign Sparse Attention): "Native sparse attention is a promising recent alternative to global self-attention, but has not been validated at the scale of frontier pretrained model releases." Limitations: "We report results with one model, distilled from one other model, and evaluated three datasets."
3. 2508.15884 (Jet-Nemotron): "architectures designed within this framework may not yield optimal results when trained from scratch" - PostNAS-derived hybrids are only validated as conversions.
4. 2504.14366 (What Matters in Linearizing): "it is unknown how linearization is affected by parameter and token budget scaling" (tested only to 1.7B).
5. 2503.05029 (CPT of MoEs): "early MoE layers change the most during CPT, suggesting that future work could investigate special treatment of these layers for improved performance."
6. 2502.03009 (Scaling laws for upcycling): "there is a novel interaction term between the dense and upcycled training dataset that limits the efficiency of upcycling at large computational budgets."
7. 2607.16062 (Merging vs joint RL): "in the reinforcement-learning setting this substitution is essentially never tested against the baseline it claims to replace: methods merge independently released agents precisely because a joint model is unavailable."
8. 2608.19098 (Open-MOPD): "the optimization dynamics governing multi-teacher capability integration remain poorly understood, and open, rigorously reproducible recipes are conspicuously lacking."
9. 2604.13016 (Rethinking OPD): "a stronger teacher can completely fail to improve a student, even when a weaker teacher succeeds from lower initial alignment. Yet few studies have investigated why."
10. 2602.12125 (ExOPD, future work): "validating the generalizability of ExOPD on larger-scale models; assessing the robustness of ExOPD in multi-teacher distillation with a broader and more diverse set of domain teachers; evaluating ... across different model families."
11. zai-blog:glm-5.3: "These pipelines still require a meaningful amount of human-in-the-loop work; making environment generation and verification more autonomous is one of the next steps."
12. 2512.13961 (Olmo 3): "all leading open RLVR benchmarks and algorithms train on top of open-weight models that do not reveal their pretraining or mid-training data. This limits the community's ability to study the role of pretraining data on RLVR performance."
13. 2510.06826 (Mid-training survey): "scheduler design remains largely empirical: the optimal duration and shape of decay vary across model sizes, architectures, and optimizers."
14. 2603.09938 (Merging survey): open challenges "including theoretical gaps, scalability barriers, and standardization needs."
15. 2509.22935 (Compute-optimal QAT): "previous assumptions about optimal QAT allocation do not hold as compute budgets increase" - optimal QAT fraction at frontier token budgets is extrapolated, not measured (largest model 2.2B).
16. 2502.20082 (LongRoPE2) and 2504.06214 (UltraLong): peer-reviewed position-interpolation evidence stops at 8B; no controlled study of YaRN-successor curricula at 100B+ was found in the window - the only 100B+ evidence is lab reports (Qwen2.5-1M, GLM-5.2, Nemotron 3 Ultra, DeepSeek-V4).

## Negative or null results

- Expert merging fails on generative tasks: REAP (2510.13999) proves merging "introduces an irreducible error ... functional subspace collapse"; HC-SMoE falls to 65.2% of baseline code quality at 50% compression on Qwen3-30B-A3B versus 95.9% for pruning.
- Naive efficient-attention conversion: GLM-5 (2602.15763) reports interleaved SWA "-30.35 on RULER@128K" and a residual 5.69/7.33-point gap for all searched SWA/GDN conversions; only DSA is lossless. Prior linearizations "often fail to match ... their teacher LLMs" (2603.15590).
- Mamba head / head-dimension pruning "caused severe accuracy degradation" (Nemotron-H, 2504.03624); depth below 56 layers degrades sharply (Nemotron Nano 2, 2508.14444).
- Upcycling at large budgets: the dense/upcycled interaction term limits efficiency (2502.03009); merging methods "lie between the pretrained model and the individually fine-tuned models" and sophisticated methods can underperform Task Arithmetic (MergeBench 2505.10833 as reported in 2603.09938 / ACM CSUR survey).
- Prolonged RL saturates after thousands of steps (BroRL 2510.01180); standard multi-teacher OPD recovers only 35.6% of the attainable gap (2608.19098); a stronger OPD teacher can fail entirely (2604.13016).
- Post-hoc MTP heads on a frozen backbone hit an NTP-specialization barrier that joint training "cannot fully overcome" (2502.09419, Mehra et al., not abs-verified; corroborated by the gated-LoRA design in 2507.11851 where ungated LoRA degrades accuracy).
- Finetuning alone is not the cheapest route: "those who rely on finetuning alone need a larger model to match domain performance" (2603.16177).
- Training-free conversion has limits: TransMLA needs recovery training beyond ~69% KV compression; ReplaceMe needs healing at high ratios (2505.02819).
- Labelled baseline (outside window): Sparse Upcycling: Inference Inefficient Finetuning (2411.08968) shows upcycled MoEs cost more at inference than dense CPT for equal quality.
