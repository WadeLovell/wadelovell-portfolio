# Unexplored Methods for Training Frontier Models: Findings

**Author:** B. Wade Lovell
**Executed:** September 5, 2026
**Evidence window:** December 31, 2024 through September 5, 2026
**Method:** `01-research-plan.md`. Sources: `references.md`. Audit trail: `search-log.md`.

## 1. Thesis

The frontier converged in 2026 on one training recipe per path, and the unexplored methods are the ones that carry peer-reviewed evidence at academic scale and sit outside that recipe. Three independent labs, Moonshot, DeepSeek, and Zhipu, disclosed the same post-training pipeline within five months of one another: a supervised cold start, reinforcement learning that produces domain experts at several reasoning-effort levels, and on-policy distillation that consolidates the experts into one model (Kimi Team, 2026; DeepSeek-AI, 2026; Z.ai, 2026b). The same three labs converged on a Muon-family optimizer, an attention stack that is sparse or partly linear, a residual-stream upgrade, an ultra-sparse mixture of experts with bias-based load balancing, a multi-token-prediction head kept for speculative decoding, four-bit quantization-aware training in post-training, and a late-stage curriculum to one million tokens of context. Convergence of that breadth makes absence measurable. A method with strong evidence that every converged recipe omits is a gap with a specific address, and that address is the finding this report delivers.

The rival reading judges novelty by recency: the newest arXiv method is the unexplored one. That reading fails the frontier test twice. Many recent methods are already inside the converged recipe under a lab's own name, so recency overstates the gap. Many older methods carry NeurIPS or ICML evidence at ten-billion-parameter scale and appear in no recipe at all, so recency understates the gap. The two-register method of the plan replaces recency with a matrix: rows are methods with Tier A or Tier B evidence, columns are frontier models, and a row with an "absent" or "undisclosed" cell in every column is a gap. Each gap then receives a grade from G1 (proposed and untested at scale) through G4 (untested combination of adopted methods), and the report ranks the graded gaps by the strength of their evidence and the size of the claimed gain.

## 2. The converged frontier recipe

### 2.1 Pretraining

Kimi K3 trains a 2.8-trillion-parameter model with 104 billion active parameters on a stack of 69 Kimi Delta Attention layers and 24 gated multi-head latent attention layers, with Attention Residuals across depth, a Stable LatentMoE layer that activates 16 of 896 experts under Quantile Balancing, and a Per-Head Muon optimizer on a cosine schedule (Kimi Team, 2026). DeepSeek-V4 trains 1.6 trillion parameters with 49 billion active on interleaved Compressed Sparse Attention and Heavily Compressed Attention, Manifold-Constrained Hyper-Connections in place of plain residuals, DeepSeekMoE, and Muon with hybrid Newton-Schulz iterations on 33 trillion tokens (DeepSeek-AI, 2026). GLM-5 trains 744 billion parameters with 40 billion active on 28.5 trillion tokens and introduces DeepSeek Sparse Attention through a 20-billion-token continued-pretraining adaptation of its dense base (Zeng et al., 2026). The three recipes agree on the optimizer family, on trained sparsity in attention, on a structural change to the residual stream, on bias-based expert balancing, and on a retained multi-token-prediction head. They agree as well on where four-bit precision enters: Kimi K3 applies MXFP4 weights and MXFP8 activations from supervised fine-tuning through reinforcement learning, and DeepSeek-V4 applies FP4 quantization-aware training to expert weights and the indexer path during post-training.

Two negative results from the Kimi K3 report matter for the gap analysis. Under independent scaling-law searches for each schedule, cosine decay reached a lower final loss than warmup-stable-decay at every configuration tested, so the report adopts cosine as the default (Kimi Team, 2026, Section 3.2). Top-k on-policy distillation objectives gave no advantage in convergence speed or final performance over the per-token log-ratio reward, so the report keeps the simpler objective (Kimi Team, 2026, Section 4.1.3). Both results are Tier C and rest on one lab's runs. Each one demotes an academic method from "absent" to "tested and rejected at frontier scale," which the gap matrix records as a distinct cell value.

### 2.2 Post-training

The consolidated pipeline runs the same way at all three labs. Kimi K3 trains nine expert policies, three domains crossed with three reasoning-effort levels, then distils them into one student with a per-token reward equal to the clipped log-ratio of teacher to student probability, a signal that rides on the same partial-rollout reinforcement-learning infrastructure as the expert stage (Kimi Team, 2026). DeepSeek-V4 cultivates domain experts through supervised fine-tuning and GRPO and consolidates them through on-policy distillation (DeepSeek-AI, 2026). GLM-5.2 merges more than ten expert models through parallel on-policy distillation in about two days on the slime framework, and GLM-5.3 reuses the GLM-5.2 base and attributes every gain to further post-training on the same stack (Z.ai, 2026a; Z.ai, 2026b). Effort control enters as a reward term: Kimi K3 overrides the task reward with a penalty when output exceeds a per-problem budget scaled by a multiplier that is annealed per domain, and GLM-5.2 and GLM-5.3 expose low, high, and max effort levels trained the same way.

Reward sources converge as well. Verifiable tasks use binary verifiers, and GLM-5.3 synthesizes verifiers without access to reference solutions and screens them with oracle, no-op, and unsolved-state checks. Open-ended tasks use an agentic generative reward model that reads the output, writes a rubric, scores each candidate against it, and records the scores, with a verbosity cap that forfeits a comparison when a candidate exceeds a length multiple (Kimi Team, 2026). GLM-5.2 adds an anti-hack module that filters tool calls by rule, judges intent with a language model, blocks the call, and returns dummy output so the rollout continues rather than collapsing the batch (Z.ai, 2026a). Long-horizon trajectories change the optimizer: GLM-5.2 replaces group-relative advantages with a critic-based proximal policy optimization on individual rollouts so that compaction sub-traces of variable count and length can all be trained (Z.ai, 2026a), and Kimi K3 tolerates extreme staleness from partial rollouts through a per-token regularizer that keeps updates in a local neighborhood (Kimi Team, 2026).

The one open problem the converged labs name in their own words concerns environments rather than algorithms. The GLM-5.3 post states that the environment-synthesis pipelines "still require a meaningful amount of human-in-the-loop work" and that "making environment generation and verification more autonomous is one of the next steps" (Z.ai, 2026b). Section 6 grades that statement as a G3 gap and pairs it with the peer-reviewed self-play and automatic-curriculum literature.

## 3. Path 1: from scratch

The 2026 flagships adopted the optimizer, attention, residual, and routing innovations of 2025 within a year of their preprints, and the peer-reviewed record now holds a second layer of results, on hyperparameter transfer, schedules, checkpoint merging, memory, and training objectives, that the flagships have yet to touch. The sweep verified 82 sources on this path: 39 at Tier A, 3 at Tier B, 17 at Tier C, and 22 at Tier D. Muon runs Kimi K2 and K3, DeepSeek-V4, and GLM-5 (Kimi Team, 2025; Kimi Team, 2026; DeepSeek-AI, 2026; GLM-5 Team, 2026). Native Sparse Attention won the ACL 2025 best paper and became DeepSeek Sparse Attention (Yuan et al., 2025, ACL). Mixture of Block Attention serves Kimi's long context (Lu et al., 2025, NeurIPS). Gated attention won a NeurIPS 2025 best paper and ships in Qwen3-Next (Qiu et al., 2025a, NeurIPS). Global-batch load balancing ships in Qwen3's mixture of experts (Qiu et al., 2025b, ACL). Latent mixture of experts ships in Kimi K3 and Nemotron 3 (Elango et al., 2026, Tier C). The efficient-MoE scaling law that predicted a sevenfold leverage at 3 percent activation ships in Ling 2.0 (Tian et al., 2026, ICLR). Those adoptions show the pipeline from peer review to flagship working at a lag of six to twelve months. The gaps below are the results that lag still holds.

**Matrix-preconditioned optimizers keep their speedup only under hyperparameter transfer that the frontier does by heuristic (G4).** Muon, SOAP, and Shampoo hold a consistent 1.4 times speedup over AdamW from 190 million to 1.4 billion parameters under maximal-update parameterization with weight decay scaled as one over width, and the speedup vanishes with scale under standard parameterization (Qiu et al., 2025c, NeurIPS). The same paper shows that matching Muon's update RMS to Adam's, the heuristic that Moonlight introduced and that Kimi K3 and DeepSeek-V4 inherit, is an incorrect width scaling. An independent benchmark at up to 8 times the Chinchilla token budget finds the matrix-preconditioned advantage decaying from 1.4 times at 100 million parameters to 1.1 times at 1.2 billion, with a fitted law that predicts Muon losing to AdamW at 7 billion parameters and one times Chinchilla (Wen et al., 2026, ICLR). NVIDIA's frontier-scale study reports the opposite at 72 billion parameters, where Muon and SOAP keep token efficiency at 100-million-token batches that degrade AdamW, and names sharding of fine-grained MoEs as the scalability hurdle (Khona et al., 2026, Tier C). The flagships settle the question by running Muon at 1.6 to 2.8 trillion parameters. What they leave untested is the Tier A prescription: a parameterization-correct transfer of learning rate and weight decay for a matrix-preconditioned optimizer at that scale, and a measurement of whether the 1.4 times survives there.

**Three peer-reviewed optimizer refinements sit below 6 billion parameters (G1).** Grouping attention heads six to a block beats both full-matrix Muon and the fully per-head split, and per-head wins early and loses later (Zhang et al., 2026, ICML). Kimi K3 uses Per-Head Muon and GLM-5 uses MuonSplit, both the per-head variant. Normalizing Muon's update per neuron improves training efficiency over Muon by 11 to 14 percent at 1.1 and 5.4 billion parameters (Li et al., 2026a, ICML). Optimal polynomial matrix-sign iterations converge about twice as fast as Newton-Schulz and lower validation loss across learning rates (Amsel et al., 2026, ICLR); DeepSeek-V4 uses a hybrid Newton-Schulz and Kimi K3 uses per-head Newton-Schulz. A Tier D spectral study of Muon at up to 2.8 billion parameters finds that late layers fall into the Newton-Schulz failure regime at frontier scale unless they receive more iterations, so the iteration count is a layer-wise choice that no flagship reports making.

**Depth-wise hyperparameter transfer is untested in any state-of-the-art model (G1).** CompleteP transfers hyperparameters from 2 to 128 layers and saves 12 to 34 percent of FLOPs over maximal-update parameterization, with the larger saving at 179 layers (Dey et al., 2025, NeurIPS). Its authors tested up to 1.5 billion non-embedding parameters and state that no state-of-the-art model has used it. Kimi K3 runs 93 layers and DeepSeek-V4-Pro runs 61, and both retune hyperparameters by in-house scaling-law sweeps (Kimi Team, 2026; DeepSeek-AI, 2026). Weight decay and batch size have their own Tier A power laws, in which the optimal decay follows a power of tokens per parameter and the critical batch size scales as the square root of data, independent of model size (Bergsma et al., 2025a, NeurIPS). The flagships disclose a weight decay of 0.1 and a warmup of 1 percent. The laws that would set both are published and unapplied.

**The learning-rate schedule the frontier chose lost to a schedule it never tested (G2).** Kimi K3 compared cosine decay with warmup-stable-decay under independent scaling-law searches and adopted cosine (Kimi Team, 2026). Linear decay to zero beats cosine, tenfold decay, and warmup-stable-decay, and the advantage grows with tokens per parameter: a 610-million-parameter model at 80 tokens per parameter with decay to zero matches 200 tokens per parameter with tenfold decay, about 60 percent of the compute (Bergsma et al., 2025b, ICLR). A multi-power law predicts loss under unseen schedules and yields an optimized schedule that beats cosine and grid-tuned warmup-stable-decay (Luo et al., 2025, ICLR). Both results are Tier A and stop below 2 billion parameters. Kimi K3's comparison is Tier C, covers two schedules, and stops where the third would begin.

**Checkpoint merging during pretraining has Tier A evidence at frontier-relevant scale and no flagship disclosure (G2).** Merging checkpoints from a constant-learning-rate run matches or beats an annealed model and predicts the annealed result, across dense models from 411 million to 70 billion parameters and mixtures of experts up to 200 billion total parameters on 16 trillion tokens (Li et al., 2025, NeurIPS). The method is ByteDance's in-house practice. Kimi K3, DeepSeek-V4, and GLM-5 each run a cooldown and say nothing about merging, so the cell reads "undisclosed." A NeurIPS 2025 method at 200 billion parameters is the closest thing in this register to a frontier-ready gap.

**Data-constrained recipes are ICLR 2026 orals at 1.4 billion parameters, and the frontier is at 33 trillion tokens (G1).** Under fixed data, a joint recipe of regularization and ensembling reaches the asymptote of a 200-million-token corpus with 5.2 times less data, a distilled student keeps 83 percent of the ensemble gain, and the recipe gives 17.5 times data efficiency on mathematics mid-training (Kim et al., 2026, ICLR). Diffusion language models tolerate about 500 epochs of repetition where autoregressive models tolerate 15 to 30, and the compute at which diffusion wins scales as the 2.2 power of unique tokens (Prabhudesai et al., 2025, NeurIPS). Synthetic rephrasing at trillion-token scale adds 2.6 points over the best prior synthetic corpus at 8 billion parameters and saturates in rephraser quality at 3 billion (Tier D). DeepSeek-V4 pretrains on 33 trillion tokens and GLM-5 on 28.5 trillion, so the flagships sit at the edge of the unique-token supply the data-constrained literature models. None discloses an epoch count, an ensemble, or a repetition regime.

**Alternative objectives have oral-track evidence below 1 billion parameters and one named compute ceiling (G1).** Energy-based transformers scale up to 35 percent faster than the strongest transformer baseline across data, batch, parameters, and depth, and gain 29 percent more from inference-time thinking, at up to 800 million parameters (Gladstone et al., 2026, ICLR). Joint-embedding prediction lifts pretraining accuracy from 54 to 61 percent on a synthetic corpus at about twice the compute (Huang et al., 2026, ICLR). The 8-billion-parameter diffusion model trained from scratch on 2.3 trillion tokens matches Llama-3 8B in in-context learning, and its authors state that every comparison with autoregressive models stayed below 10 to the 23 FLOPs (Nie et al., 2025, NeurIPS). Reinforcement as a pretraining objective adds 19 percent on eight benchmarks at 1.7 billion parameters and raises a 12-billion hybrid from 43 to 61 (Hatamizadeh et al., 2026, ICLR), and NVIDIA lists it in the Nemotron line. Gemini Diffusion and Mercury exist at undisclosed scale (Tier C and D). The frontier flagships are autoregressive next-token predictors with one multi-token head, and the 10-to-the-23 ceiling is the highest compute at which anyone has published a controlled alternative.

**Memory and state-space layers have Tier A results the same labs left out of their flagships (G2).** Conditional memory through scalable lookup adds 3.4 points of MMLU and 5 points of BBH at 27 billion parameters at equal parameters and FLOPs against a mixture of experts, with a 100-billion-parameter table offloaded to the host at under 3 percent overhead (Cheng et al., 2026, ACL). The work is DeepSeek's, and DeepSeek-V4 discloses compressed attention, hyper-connections, and Muon with no memory layer. Test-time memorization modules scale needle retrieval past 2 million tokens at 760 million parameters (Behrouz et al., 2025, NeurIPS). Mamba-3 improves on Gated DeltaNet by 0.6 to 1.8 points at 1.5 billion parameters and halves the state size (Lahoti et al., 2026, ICLR); Nemotron 3 uses Mamba-2 and Kimi K3 uses a delta-rule descendant. The residual stream is where the frontier moved fastest, with Attention Residuals at Kimi and manifold-constrained hyper-connections at DeepSeek (Chen et al., 2026, Tier C; Xie et al., 2025, Tier C), and both are Tier C at 27 to 48 billion parameters in their own ablations.

**Low-precision pretraining has two Tier A laws and one production run that disagree about the data limit (G3).** Floating-point quantization scaling laws identify a critical data size beyond which low-precision loss rises and place the best cost-performance precision at 4 to 8 bits (Sun et al., 2025, ICML). Native MXFP4 training is near-lossless in the large-data regime at up to 1.6 billion parameters with a 7-billion stability run (Castro et al., 2025, NeurIPS). Nemotron 3 Super is the first production model pretrained in NVFP4, on 25 trillion tokens at 120 billion parameters, with a loss gap under 0.4 percent to BF16 (NVIDIA, 2026b, Tier C). The Tier A law predicts a penalty at that token count. The Tier C run reports none. The disagreement is measurable and unmeasured in public.

**Mid-training and data-mixture laws are peer-reviewed at 7 billion parameters and disclosed in outline above it (G2).** A scaling law fitted at small scale predicts loss at 8 billion parameters and on unseen mixtures, and law-derived weights beat token-proportional and uniform mixtures downstream (Shukor et al., 2025, NeurIPS). Mid-training helps most for code and mathematics, and its timing interacts with its mixture: an early start tolerates 80 percent specialized data and a late start needs about 10 percent (Liu et al., 2026, ICML). A Tier D study across 3 to 24 billion parameters finds mid-training restructuring more than 90 percent of the weights while reinforcement learning changes about 5 percent, and finds reinforcement learning succeeding only after mid-training. Every flagship describes a mid-training stage by its context lengths and token counts. Its mixture weights, its timing rule, and the law that set them stay in-house.

## 4. Path 2: from or through distilled models

On-policy distillation moved from a proposal to the consolidation stage of every disclosed frontier recipe within twelve months, and the peer-reviewed literature now documents four properties of that method that no frontier report addresses. The sweep verified 88 sources on this path: 20 at Tier A, 1 at Tier B, 18 at Tier C, and 47 at Tier D, with 31 newer than May 2026. Kimi K3 discloses multi-teacher on-policy distillation from nine expert policies, DeepSeek-V4 discloses on-policy distillation as its consolidation stage, GLM-5.2 discloses a parallel on-policy distillation merge of more than ten experts, Nemotron 3 Ultra discloses two iterations of multi-teacher distillation from more than ten domain teachers with a warm-up stage, and MiMo-V2-Flash and Nemotron-Cascade 2 disclose the same pattern at smaller scale (Kimi Team, 2026; DeepSeek-AI, 2026; Z.ai, 2026a; NVIDIA, 2026a; Xiaomi, 2026; NVIDIA, 2026b). Kimi K2.5 uses the word distillation nowhere, and its supervised data comes from earlier Kimi models and in-house experts, so "distilled model" in the sense of the user's second path describes Kimi K3 rather than its predecessor. The method is adopted. The gaps sit in what the adopters leave unmeasured.

**Scaling laws for on-policy distillation are a named open problem (G3).** A distillation scaling law exists for offline distillation and fits student cross-entropy as a function of student size, distillation tokens, and teacher cross-entropy across students and teachers from 143 million to 12.6 billion parameters and up to 512 billion tokens (Busbridge et al., 2025, ICML). That law shows distillation beating supervised pretraining only below a student-size-dependent compute threshold and only when the teacher already exists. No equivalent law exists for on-policy distillation, where student rollouts add a compute axis, and the survey of the field states the unanswered question directly: whether more on-policy data can compensate for a weaker teacher or teacher quality sets a hard ceiling (Song & Zheng, 2026, survey, Tier D). Three labs now spend two days to two iterations of frontier compute on this stage with no published law to size it.

**Cross-family teachers break the adopted method, and the peer-reviewed fix is untested above 8 billion parameters (G1).** Multi-teacher distillation from a Qwen3-235B teacher into a Qwen3-30B student diverges under the top-k objective and degrades under the policy-gradient objective, so same-origin teachers are required in practice (Ma et al., 2026, ICML workshop, Tier D). Every frontier consolidation stage distils within one model family for that reason. Approximate likelihood matching over chunk alignments distils across fundamentally different tokenizers, including subword to byte, at 7 to 8 billion parameters (Minixhofer et al., 2025, NeurIPS), and a 2026 extension carries it to the on-policy setting (Tier D). A frontier lab that wants a stronger external teacher, or a student with a new tokenizer, has a Tier A method and no report of anyone trying it at scale.

**Entropy-aware and self-distilled objectives carry ICML 2026 evidence at 4 to 8 billion parameters and appear in no recipe (G1).** Entropy-aware on-policy distillation applies reverse KL on low-entropy teacher tokens and forward KL over the teacher's top-k where teacher entropy is high, and raises pass@8 by 1.4 to 5.1 points over standard on-policy distillation on students up to 4 billion parameters (Jin et al., 2026, ICML). On-policy self-distillation makes one model both teacher, conditioned on the reference solution, and student, and reaches four to eight times the token efficiency of GRPO on students up to 8 billion parameters (Zhao et al., 2026, ICML). Privileged-information distillation trains the conditioned teacher and the unconditioned student with shared parameters and beats supervised-then-RL pipelines on agentic benchmarks at 7 billion parameters (Penaloza et al., 2026, ICML). Kimi K3 tested a top-k objective, a different variant, and found no advantage over the sampled-token log-ratio reward (Kimi Team, 2026). That single Tier C null result leaves the entropy-gated mixture and the privileged self-teacher untested at frontier scale, and a Tier D study reports that privileged self-distillation degrades thinking models by up to 17 percent on competition mathematics, so the risk on the self-teacher branch is documented as well (Tier D).

**On-policy distillation degrades along long trajectories, and frontier reports apply it to agentic tasks without a stated remedy (G3).** The teacher signal on the last 30 percent of rollout positions teaches almost nothing, local teachability collapses under strong-to-weak distillation, and the dense token reward decays with trajectory depth (Tier D, three independent studies). The survey lists "systematic degradation of teacher supervision with trajectory depth" as a foundational open problem and proposes hybrids of dense token supervision on short segments with sparse outcome rewards on long horizons (Tier D). Kimi K3 runs multi-teacher distillation on long-horizon agentic experts through partial rollouts and reports the consolidated result with no position-wise analysis. The peer-reviewed side has the diagnosis at small scale. The frontier side has the deployment at large scale. Neither has the cure.

**Whether on-policy distillation expands capability at all is contested (G3).** Across teacher-student pairs up to 1024 samples per problem, on-policy distillation raises average accuracy and leaves the pass@1024 boundary at the pre-distillation base, while off-policy distillation expands that boundary (Tier D). A related ICML 2025 result shows that supervised fine-tuning memorizes and reinforcement learning generalizes out of distribution, with supervised data needed only to stabilize format before RL (Chu et al., 2025, ICML). Distillation raises pass@k only when the teacher supplies new knowledge, and pattern-only distillation behaves like RL with verifiable rewards (Tier D). The frontier consolidates capabilities that already exist in the experts, which is the case where the contested result is harmless. Using distillation to lift a student above its teachers' union is the case the evidence leaves open.

**Multi-teacher imbalance and teacher hacking have Tier A diagnoses and Tier D fixes (G1).** Teacher hacking, where the student's distance to the teacher falls while its distance to the oracle rises, appears with fixed offline teacher data and disappears with as little as 10 percent online data or with prompt diversity (Tiapkin et al., 2025, ICML). The frontier's on-policy setting therefore avoids it by construction, which counts as implicit adoption. The multi-teacher setting adds a new failure: standard multi-teacher distillation recovers 35.6 percent of the oracle ensemble headroom on a 3-billion-parameter student, and token-share balancing with gap-aware budgets raises that to 83.4 percent (Tier D, August 2026). Nemotron 3 Ultra inserts a warm-up supervised stage and two teacher co-evolution iterations to manage the same imbalance and gives no analysis of how many iterations help (NVIDIA, 2026a). The fix is one month old, sits at 3 billion parameters, and addresses a stage that three frontier labs run.

**Distillation as a pretraining route stops at the small end of the frontier (G4).** Gemma 3, Apple's 2025 foundation models, and Llama 4's smaller members disclose distillation from a larger teacher during pretraining (Google, 2025; Apple, 2025; Meta, 2025). Kimi K3, DeepSeek-V4, and GLM-5 disclose supervised pretraining from scratch for their flagships. The distillation scaling law predicts that outcome below 12.6 billion parameters and says nothing above it (Busbridge et al., 2025, ICML), and a Tier D study finds that teacher-student compatibility rather than teacher strength governs pretraining distillation gains at up to 8 billion parameters. Whether a compatible frontier teacher could pretrain a frontier-scale student below the supervised cost is a combination of two adopted ingredients, distillation pretraining and trillion-parameter training, that no report evaluates.

**Distilled bases as reinforcement-learning starting points carry conflicting Tier A and Tier C evidence (G3).** The Nature version of DeepSeek-R1 reports that distilling its traces into small models beats running RL on those models directly (DeepSeek-AI, 2025, Nature). Magistral reports that pure RL on a 24-billion-parameter base matches distillation on competition mathematics and that RL on top of the distilled checkpoint scores highest (Mistral, 2025, Tier C). Both results sit below 30 billion parameters, and the frontier labs run RL on natively pretrained bases and distil afterward, so the ordering question at frontier scale, distil then RL or RL then distil, remains a matter of lab convention rather than measured evidence.

## 5. Path 3: from leading open-weights models

The open-weights bases of 2026 are already mixture-of-experts models with sparse or hybrid attention, so the productive interventions on this path change what a released checkpoint can do at a fraction of its pretraining cost, and the peer-reviewed record shows five such interventions validated between 8 and 1,000 billion parameters that no frontier recipe applies. The sweep verified 95 sources on this path: 27 at Tier A, 10 at Tier B, 20 at Tier C, and 38 at Tier D. The frontier adopts four adaptations of a released base. DeepSeek-V3.2 converts a 685-billion-parameter dense-attention checkpoint to DeepSeek Sparse Attention through 943.7 billion tokens of continued pretraining, and GLM-5 reaches the same result on its own base in 1,000 warm-up steps and 20 billion tokens (DeepSeek-AI, 2025; GLM-5 Team, 2026). Llama-Nemotron prunes Llama 3.1-405B to 253 billion parameters, recovers with 153 billion tokens, and then exceeds the source through reinforcement learning (Bercovich et al., 2025, Tier C). Olmo 3, DAPO, and ProRL run reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards on open bases with full disclosure (Team OLMo, 2025, Tier C; Yu et al., 2025, NeurIPS; Liu et al., 2025, NeurIPS). GLM-5.3 reuses the GLM-5.2 base and raises Terminal-Bench 3.0 from 4.6 to 28.3 through post-training alone (Z.ai, 2026b). Those four adaptations define "explored." The five below define the gap.

**Conversion to linear or hybrid attention is validated at 72 billion parameters and rejected at one frontier lab on evidence the peer-reviewed fix addresses (G2).** A three-stage protocol of attention-weight transfer, hidden-state alignment, and distribution matching converts Qwen2.5 models at 7, 32, and 72 billion parameters to linear-attention decoders with 350 to 700 million tokens, under 0.005 percent of the teacher's pretraining tokens, at a quality that stays close to the source (Goldstein et al., 2025, COLM). Post-training neural architecture search freezes the multilayer perceptrons of a pretrained model, learns where full attention must remain, and delivers a 2-billion-parameter hybrid that matches Qwen3-1.7B with 53.6 times the generation throughput at 256K context (Gu et al., 2025, NeurIPS). Gate construction from pretrained key projections recovers 93 percent of Llama-3-8B with 0.02 percent of its tokens (Lan et al., 2025, ICML). GLM-5 ran the conversion ablations, found a residual gap of 5.7 to 7.3 points for every sliding-window and gated-delta conversion it tried, and adopted sparse attention instead (GLM-5 Team, 2026). The residual gap has a Tier A diagnosis: which layers keep softmax attention governs the outcome, and scoring each layer by the KL reduction from restoring it beats fixed-ratio interleaving on long-context retrieval (Li et al., 2026, ICLR). That selection method was tested at 3 billion parameters. The frontier ran the conversion at 744 billion parameters without it.

**Native sparse attention by distillation has Tier B evidence at 1.5 billion parameters and a limitation statement that names the frontier directly (G1).** A four-stage distillation converts a global-attention reasoner into Native Sparse Attention with 100 million tokens and matches the teacher while generating fewer tokens, and its authors write that the architecture "has not been validated at the scale of frontier pretrained model releases" (Sundarapandiyan et al., 2025, Tier B). DeepSeek and Zhipu convert to their own sparse-attention designs. Nobody has reported converting an open frontier base to the peer-reviewed alternative.

**Expert pruning is validated at one trillion parameters, and the frontier keeps the experts (G2).** Router-weighted expert saliency prunes 50 percent of the experts from Qwen3-Coder-480B and Kimi-K2 with at most a 2 percent drop on code generation, and the same paper proves that expert merging causes functional subspace collapse on generative tasks (Lasby et al., 2026, ICLR). That is Tier A evidence at frontier scale, produced outside the frontier labs. Every disclosed 2026 flagship ships its full expert set, and the one lab that prunes flagships, NVIDIA, prunes dense and hybrid Mamba models rather than experts (Bercovich et al., 2025; NVIDIA, 2025, Tier C). A frontier-class model at half the expert memory sits one peer-reviewed step from an existing checkpoint.

**Growth from a checkpoint has Tier A and Tier B recipes below 10 billion parameters and no frontier trial (G1).** Drop-upcycling re-initializes part of each expert while upcycling a dense checkpoint and reaches 13-billion-dense quality with 5.9 billion active parameters at a quarter of the training compute (Nakamura et al., 2025, ICLR). Scaling laws for upcycling then identify an interaction term between the dense and upcycled datasets that limits efficiency at large budgets (Liew et al., 2025, ICML). Depth up-scaling with layers predicted from the singular-value structure of existing layers beats copy-based scaling at under half the continued-pretraining compute (Yang et al., 2025, ACL), and optimal-transport alignment of adjacent blocks improves progressive depth growth further (Cao et al., 2025, Tier B). Frontier bases are already MoE, so the relevant growth is expert expansion or depth growth of a released MoE, and the Tier D evidence for that case stops at 13 billion parameters. The interaction term is a named limit with no measurement above the academic scale.

**Continued pretraining of a mixture of experts has one TMLR recipe and one named open problem (G3).** Across 600 billion tokens, MoE routers balanced by Sinkhorn or penalty terms tolerate distribution shift without replay, and replay with an infinite learning-rate schedule matches full retraining at a fraction of the cost (Therien et al., 2025, TMLR). The same study finds that early MoE layers change the most during continued pretraining and proposes special treatment of those layers as future work. Scaling laws for forgetting under pretraining-data injection make the replay ratio predictable (Bethune et al., 2025, Tier B), and self-generated samples serve as replay data that nearly eliminates forgetting (Tier D). GLM-5's 20-billion-token adaptation discloses the token count and the learning rate and nothing about replay, so the frontier cell reads "undisclosed" rather than "absent." The layer-specific treatment is a G3 gap with a Tier A source.

**Merging reinforcement-learned experts is Tier A evidence for a step the frontier skips (G4).** Experts trained by reinforcement learning merge with less interference than experts trained by supervised fine-tuning, because their task vectors are closer to orthogonal (Ren et al., 2026, ICLR). Merging methods otherwise land between the base model and the individual fine-tunes, and sophisticated methods can lose to task arithmetic (He et al., 2025, Tier B). A controlled comparison at 30 billion parameters found multi-teacher on-policy distillation beating parameter merging by 5.5 normalized points (Ma et al., 2026, Tier D), and the frontier consolidates by distillation rather than merging for that reason. The untested combination is merging as a cheap initialization before distillation, which would start the student closer to the teachers' union at zero rollout cost. No report evaluates it, and a Tier D study notes that merging "is essentially never tested against the baseline it claims to replace" in the reinforcement-learning setting (McClendon, 2026).

**Quantization-aware training allocation has a Tier B law that contradicts the frontier's fixed rule (G3).** Across 757 runs up to 2.2 billion parameters, the optimal fraction of training spent under quantization grows with tokens per parameter-byte, the common "10 percent" rule fails, and fusing the cooldown with quantization-aware training reaches the same loss at about half the compute at low bit widths (Dremov et al., 2025, Tier B). A second law over 268 runs finds W4A4 error falling with model size and rising with token count and group size (Chen et al., 2026, ICML). Kimi K3 runs quantization-aware training from supervised fine-tuning onward, DeepSeek-V4 runs it in post-training, and Nemotron 3 Ultra pretrains in NVFP4 with under 0.4 percent loss gap to BF16 (Kimi Team, 2026; DeepSeek-AI, 2026; NVIDIA, 2026a). Each lab picked a fraction by convention. The law that would size it is extrapolated from 2.2 billion parameters, and its authors say the earlier assumptions "do not hold as compute budgets increase."

**Long-context extension has Tier A evidence at 8 billion parameters and undisclosed recipes above it (G2).** Evolutionary rescaling of under-trained rotary dimensions with mixed-window training reaches 128K effective context on Llama-3-8B with 10 billion tokens, 80 times fewer than Meta's recipe (Shang et al., 2025, ICML). A continued-pretraining recipe with document separators reaches 4 million tokens on the same base (Xu et al., 2025, Tier C), and interleaving position-free global layers with windowed rotary layers converts an existing model at about 2 percent of its pretraining compute and generalizes beyond the training length (Puvvada et al., 2025, EMNLP). Kimi K3, DeepSeek-V4, and GLM-5.2 reach one million tokens through late-stage curricula whose rescaling method and token counts appear in outline only. The peer-reviewed extension methods stop at 8 billion parameters. The frontier methods stop at the outline.

**Reinforcement learning touches a small subnetwork, and nobody exploits that at frontier scale (G4).** Across seven algorithms and ten models, reinforcement learning updates 5 to 30 percent of parameters, and the subnetworks overlap across seeds and algorithms (Mukherjee et al., 2025, NeurIPS). Low-rank adaptation matches full fine-tuning for reinforcement learning even at low rank (Schulman et al., 2025, Tier D). Every frontier post-training run disclosed in the window is full-parameter, and GLM-5.3 spends a month of post-training compute on a 744-billion-parameter base. Restricting frontier reinforcement learning to the subnetwork it would touch anyway is a combination of two documented facts that no report tests.

Two further gaps have Tier A sources and small footprints. A pretrained plug-and-play memory decoder that imitates a nearest-neighbor retriever lowers perplexity by 6.2 points across biomedicine, finance, and law with no change to the base weights (Cao et al., 2025, NeurIPS), and no frontier base ships with one. Post-hoc multi-token-prediction heads on a frozen backbone reach nearly 5 times faster code generation only with gated low-rank adapters, because ungated adapters degrade the next-token head (Tier D); the frontier keeps its heads from pretraining, so open bases without a head, the Llama line included, have a route that no lab has reported taking.

The open problem that frames the whole path comes from Olmo 3. Every open RLVR benchmark and algorithm trains on bases "that do not reveal their pretraining or mid-training data," which blocks study of how mid-training shapes what reinforcement learning can later elicit (Team OLMo, 2025, Tier C). A Tier D study finds that mid-training content bounds the reinforcement-learning gain, and a NeurIPS 2025 study across more than 100 models finds continued pretraining to be the enabler of downstream reinforcement-learning gains (Qi et al., 2025, NeurIPS). GLM-5.3 proves that post-training alone moves a frontier base a long way. The evidence that would say how far, and why, requires the disclosure that only Olmo and Nemotron provide.

## 6. Path 4: from existing frontier models through post-training

Post-training is the path where the frontier moves fastest and discloses most, and the peer-reviewed record answers three questions the flagships leave open: what raises the reinforcement-learning ceiling rather than its efficiency, which tokens carry the learning signal, and how a model can build its own training environments. The sweep verified 85 of 89 sources on this path: 30 at Tier A, 9 at Tier B, 25 at Tier C, and 25 at Tier D. The adopted recipe rests on peer-reviewed foundations. DeepSeek-R1's pure reinforcement learning on a 671-billion-parameter base is the first frontier recipe to pass peer review (Guo et al., 2025, Nature). DAPO's clip-higher, dynamic sampling, and token-level loss reach 50 on AIME 2024 from a 32-billion base and appear in GLM-5 under an upper clip of 0.28 (Yu et al., 2025, NeurIPS; GLM-5 Team, 2026). Fully asynchronous training with a staleness cap and a decoupled objective gives a 2.8 times speedup at matched accuracy and runs Ant Group's trillion-parameter models (Fu et al., 2025, NeurIPS). Rubrics generated per instance beat Likert judges by up to 31 percent on HealthBench and appear in Kimi K3's agentic reward model, which writes a rubric before it scores (Gunjal et al., 2026, ICLR; Kimi Team, 2026). On-policy data explains why reinforcement learning forgets less than supervised fine-tuning, and that finding is the stated rationale for the on-policy consolidation stage at three labs (Shenfeld et al., 2026, ICLR; Chen et al., 2026, ICML). The gaps are the results that came after those foundations.

**The interventions the frontier tunes change reinforcement-learning efficiency, and the two that raise the ceiling are the two it cannot tune cheaply (G3).** A 400,000-GPU-hour study fits sigmoid compute-performance curves to reinforcement-learning runs and finds that loss aggregation, curriculum, length penalty, and advantage normalization move the efficiency term and leave the asymptote fixed, while only context length and model size move the asymptote, at up to a 17-billion-by-16-expert mixture (Khatri et al., 2026, ICLR). Kimi K3 reports that tool-call steps and capability scale together with reinforcement-learning FLOPs, and GLM-5.3 reports a month of further post-training on a fixed base moving Terminal-Bench 3.0 from 4.6 to 28.3 (Kimi Team, 2026; Z.ai, 2026b). Neither report fits an asymptote. The Tier A result says the asymptote is the quantity that matters and names two knobs. Which knob the frontier's month of compute moved is unmeasured, and which knobs beyond those two raise the ceiling at 744 billion to 2.8 trillion parameters is untested.

**Whether reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards expands capability is contested at Tier A and unexamined at frontier scale (G3).** Base models surpass their reinforcement-learned versions at 256 samples per problem across six algorithms and two families, and distillation expands the solution boundary where reinforcement learning sharpens it (Yue et al., 2025, NeurIPS oral). Prolonged reinforcement learning past 2,000 steps with reference resets finds strategies the base never produces even under extensive sampling, at 1.5 billion parameters (Liu et al., 2025, NeurIPS). Counting correct intermediate reasoning rather than final answers restores a reinforcement-learning gain hidden by answer-only pass@k (ICLR 2026). Support shrinkage still exceeds expansion by about 3.6 to 1 even for the prolonged-training checkpoints, and the remedy named is to seed probability mass into underrepresented solution regions (Wu et al., 2025, Tier B). Qwen3 found no pass@64 gain from reinforcement learning on an 8-billion student and chose distillation at one tenth the GPU hours (Qwen Team, 2025, Tier C). No flagship publishes a pass@k boundary at large k. The debate has three Tier A entries and one Tier B entry, and the frontier's evidence is a single Tier C null result at 8 billion parameters.

**A minority of tokens carries the reinforcement-learning signal, and the frontier updates all of them (G2).** Restricting policy-gradient updates to the 20 percent of tokens with the highest entropy adds 11 points on AIME 2025 for a 32-billion base, with a gain that grows with scale (Wang et al., 2025, NeurIPS). Entropy collapse follows a law in which the change in entropy tracks the covariance of log-probability and advantage, and clipping or penalizing the high-covariance tokens adds 6.4 points over GRPO at 32 billion parameters where entropy bonuses and reference KL fail (Cui et al., 2025a, Tier B). Preserving low-probability exploratory tokens sustains exploration past the collapse plateau (Huang et al., 2026, ACL Findings), a pass@k reward with an entropy-guided switch raises pass@k without losing pass@1 on 7- and 32-billion dense and MoE models (Chen et al., 2025, Tier B), and under 4 percent of tokens recover the full reinforcement-learning gain on a 7-billion model (Tier D). Kimi K3 constrains every token through a per-token regularizer and GLM-5.2 applies a token-level loss to compaction sub-traces (Kimi Team, 2026; Z.ai, 2026a). The Tier A evidence at 32 billion parameters says the signal is sparse. The frontier objective treats it as dense.

**Self-play and environment construction are the frontier's own named open problem, with Tier A methods at 4 to 14 billion parameters (G3).** GLM-5.3 synthesizes environments and verifiers through research agents and a judge agent, and its authors state that the pipelines "still require a meaningful amount of human-in-the-loop work" and that autonomous environment generation "is one of the next steps" (Z.ai, 2026b). A single model that proposes and solves abduction, deduction, and induction tasks with a learnability reward reaches state of the art among zero-data models at 14 billion parameters (Zhao et al., 2025, NeurIPS). Self-play on zero-sum text games with role-conditioned advantages adds 10 percent across eight reasoning benchmarks at 8 billion parameters and beats supervised fine-tuning on 25,000 expert trajectories (Liu et al., 2026, ICLR). A challenger-solver pair improves a 4-billion base by 6.5 points on mathematics and collapses after about three iterations (Huang et al., 2026, ICLR). Self-play in adaptive synthetic executable environments adds 5.3 points on eight held-out benchmarks at 30 billion parameters (Tier D, August 2026). The frontier has the need, in its own words, and the peer-reviewed side has methods that collapse after three iterations at 4 billion parameters. Neither has run the other.

**Learned effort control is peer-reviewed at 1.5 to 7 billion parameters, and the frontier trains one expert per effort level (G2).** Length-controlled policy optimization lets a prompt specify the target length and beats budget forcing by more than 20 points at 1.5 billion parameters (Aggarwal & Welleck, 2025, COLM). A constrained objective that prefers no-thinking subject to accuracy cuts length by 53 percent and adds 2.4 points (Zhang et al., 2025, EMNLP), and a control token with a decoupled objective removes 50 to 90 percent of long-form reasoning on easy sets (Fang et al., 2025, NeurIPS). Kimi K3 trains nine experts, three domains by three effort levels, under a budget multiplier annealed per domain "under human-in-the-loop guidance," and distils them into one model; DeepSeek-V4 and GLM-5.3 expose fixed effort modes (Kimi Team, 2026; DeepSeek-AI, 2026; Z.ai, 2026b). A single run that learns effort control end to end is the Tier A method. Nine runs and a distillation is the frontier method.

**Dense and verifier-free rewards have Tier B evidence and one named gap, verifiable partial credit (G3).** An implicit process reward model defined as a log-ratio and updated online from outcome labels alone gives 2.5 times sample efficiency at 7 billion parameters (Cui et al., 2025b, Tier B). Self-certainty replaces the verifier and matches GRPO on mathematics with a 65 percent relative transfer gain to code (Zhao et al., 2026, ICLR), the mean token probability of a reference answer extends verifiable-reward training to general domains (Yu et al., 2025b, Tier B), and entropy minimization alone matches GRPO on Qwen and fails on Llama (Agarwal et al., 2025, NeurIPS). Random and incorrect rewards add 21 points on Qwen2.5-Math through clipping bias and add nothing on other families (Shao et al., 2026, ICML), which turns every small-model reward result into a claim that needs a family control. A survey accepted at ACM TIST names "verifiable partial credit" without the bias of learned critics as the open gap for practitioners (Srivastava & Aggarwal, 2026). Kimi K3 uses binary verifiers and a rubric-writing judge, and GLM-5.3 synthesizes binary verifiers. Partial credit is absent at every lab.

**Multi-turn credit assignment has a Tier A method at 7 billion parameters, and the frontier approximates it with a critic (G2).** Combining episode-level advantages with anchor-state step-level advantages, critic-free, adds 12 percent on ALFWorld and 9 percent on WebShop over GRPO at the same cost (Feng et al., 2025, NeurIPS). GLM-5.2 moved from group-relative advantages to a critic-based proximal policy optimization on individual rollouts so that compaction sub-traces of variable count and length become trainable, and the compaction method's own paper concedes that "cross-trajectory GAE is an approximation" (Z.ai, 2026a; Li et al., 2026b, Tier C). Turn-level critics beat token-level critics on multi-turn tasks in Tier D studies. The frontier's long-horizon objective is a Tier C approximation, and the Tier A alternative stops at 7 billion parameters.

**Forgetting and the sparsity of reinforcement-learning updates are measured at 3 to 8 billion parameters and assumed above (G3).** Forgetting is predicted by the KL divergence from the base on the new task, and on-policy reinforcement learning is implicitly KL-minimal, with the authors stating that behavior "at frontier scales and in more diverse generative domains remains unknown" (Shenfeld et al., 2026, ICLR). Reinforcement-learned task vectors are sparse, off-principal, and near-orthogonal across runs, so task arithmetic and its successors degrade merged models and a Fisher-arbitrated merge recovers them (Wu et al., 2026, KDD; Yuan et al., 2026, ACL). The frontier answers forgetting with on-policy distillation and never merges, which Path 3 records as the G4 merge-then-distil gap. The frontier answer to forgetting rests on a 3-billion-parameter measurement and a rationale.

**Test-time reinforcement learning is a NeurIPS 2025 method for a paradigm DeepSeek-V4 names as its next (G1).** Majority-vote pseudo-labels on unlabeled test prompts serve as a rule-based reward and lift pass@1 on AIME 2024 by 211 percent for a 7-billion model (Zuo et al., 2025, NeurIPS). DeepSeek-V4's introduction states that million-token context "establishes a necessary foundation for exploring future paradigms like online learning" (DeepSeek-AI, 2026). No flagship discloses any learning after deployment.

Two Tier C findings constrain every gap above. Placing a chain-of-thought monitor inside the reward produces obfuscated reward hacking and drops monitor recall toward zero, so OpenAI proposes a "monitorability tax" of withholding optimization pressure from the chain of thought (Baker et al., 2025, Tier C). Reinforcement learning on a mixture of experts diverges without routing replay under GRPO, and non-deterministic top-k in a sparse-attention indexer collapsed GLM-5's reinforcement learning within steps until a deterministic kernel replaced it (Zheng et al., 2025, Tier C; GLM-5 Team, 2026). The train-inference mismatch behind that collapse has Tier B evidence and a Tier D claim that it is a first-order factor (Yao et al., 2025, Tier B), and the frontier closed it by engineering: GLM-5.3 aligns training and rollout numerics to a log-probability difference of 10 to the minus 7, DeepSeek-V4 ships batch-invariant kernels, and Kimi K3 runs rollout and training under the same quantization (Z.ai, 2026b; DeepSeek-AI, 2026; Kimi Team, 2026). That is one gap the frontier closed ahead of peer review.

## 8. Threats to the findings

Four limits bound the register. Non-disclosure is the largest. Absence in a report means the report omits the method, and a lab that runs checkpoint merging or entropy-selective updates in-house without saying so would turn a G2 into an adoption. The gap matrix separates "absent," where the report describes the stage and omits the method, from "undisclosed," where the report says nothing about the stage, and ranks 3, 13, and 16 rest partly on undisclosed cells. Family effects are the second limit. Random rewards improve Qwen2.5-Math through clipping bias and improve nothing else (Shao et al., 2026, ICML), and small-model distillation results reverse between Qwen and Llama families, so every Tier A result below 10 billion parameters that used one family carries an unmeasured family term. Recency is the third. Twenty-one Tier B items await decisions, NeurIPS 2026 among them on September 24, 2026, and the ranking would change if the entropy-mechanism, pass@k, and implicit-process-reward submissions publish. Channel bias is the fourth. Exa and web search rank by traffic, and the sweeps corrected for it with venue filters and reference-list snowballing from six seed reports, but a method with Tier A evidence and no citations from a frontier report could still be missing.

## 9. Conclusion

The frontier converged in 2026 on one training recipe per path, and the unexplored methods are the ones that carry peer-reviewed evidence at academic scale and sit outside that recipe. The register method delivered that set. On the pretraining path, the converged recipe runs Muon-class optimizers, trained sparsity in attention, upgraded residual streams, and ultra-sparse experts, and it leaves parameterization-correct transfer, checkpoint merging, schedule choice, and memory layers on the peer-reviewed side of the line. On the distillation path, the recipe consolidates experts by on-policy distillation and leaves its scaling law, its cross-family form, and its long-horizon decay unmeasured. On the open-weights path, the recipe converts attention and prunes dense models and leaves expert pruning, layer-selected conversion, and merge-then-distil untried at the scale where peer review already tested them. On the post-training path, the recipe scales reinforcement-learning compute and synthesizes environments by hand, and it leaves the asymptote, the sparse token signal, and autonomous environment generation to the literature that has already named them. The rival reading, novelty by recency, would have surfaced the newest on-policy distillation variants and missed the NeurIPS 2025 result at 200 billion parameters that no flagship discloses. Two registers, one gap matrix, and four grades found it.
