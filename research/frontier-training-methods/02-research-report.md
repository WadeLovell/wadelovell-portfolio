# Unexplored Methods for Training Frontier Models: Findings

**Author:** B. Wade Lovell
**Executed:** September 5, 2026
**Evidence window:** December 31, 2024 through September 5, 2026
**Method:** `01-research-plan.md`. Sources: `references.md`. Audit trail: `search-log.md`.

## 1. Thesis

The frontier converged in 2026 on one training recipe per path, and the unexplored methods are the ones that carry peer-reviewed evidence at academic scale and sit outside that recipe. Three independent labs, Moonshot, DeepSeek, and Zhipu, converged in pretraining on a Muon-family optimizer, an attention stack that is sparse or partly linear, a residual-stream upgrade, and an ultra-sparse mixture of experts (MoE) with bias-based load balancing (Kimi Team, 2026; DeepSeek-AI, 2026; GLM-5 Team, 2026). Each keeps a multi-token-prediction (MTP) head for speculative decoding, applies four-bit quantization-aware training (QAT) in post-training, and extends context to one million tokens in a late-stage curriculum. The same three labs disclosed the same post-training pipeline within five months of one another: a supervised cold start, then reinforcement learning that produces domain experts at several reasoning-effort levels, then on-policy distillation (OPD) that consolidates the experts into one model (Kimi Team, 2026; DeepSeek-AI, 2026; Z.ai, 2026b). Convergence of that breadth makes absence measurable. A method with Tier A evidence that every converged recipe omits is a gap with a specific address: the stage it belongs to, the scale at which peer review tested it, and the flagship that skipped it.

The rival reading judges novelty by recency: the newest arXiv method is the unexplored one. Many recent methods already sit inside the converged recipe under a lab's own name, so recency overstates the gap. Many older methods carry NeurIPS or ICML evidence at ten-billion-parameter scale and appear in no recipe at all, so recency understates the gap. The two-register method of the plan replaces recency with a matrix. Rows are methods with Tier A or Tier B evidence. Columns are frontier models. Cells read "disclosed," "hinted," "absent," "undisclosed," or "rejected," and a row whose every cell reads "absent," "undisclosed," or "rejected" is a gap. Each gap then receives a grade: G1 for a method proposed and untested at scale, G2 for a method validated at scale and absent from frontier recipes, G3 for a named open problem, and G4 for an untested combination of adopted methods. The report ranks the graded gaps by evidence tier, by the largest scale tested, by whether every disclosed flagship runs the stage, and by whether the frontier names the gap itself. The matrix appears in Section 2.3.

## 2. The converged frontier recipe

Every source in this section is a technical report, a model card, or a release post, so every claim in it holds Tier C. The register establishes adoption. Tier A and Tier B sources establish validity in Sections 3 through 6.

### 2.1 Pretraining

Three flagship reports disclose the same five pretraining choices: the optimizer family, trained sparsity in attention, a structural change to the residual stream, bias-based expert balancing, and a retained MTP head. Kimi K3 trains a 2.8-trillion-parameter model with 104 billion active parameters on 69 Kimi Delta Attention layers and 24 gated multi-head latent attention layers (Kimi Team, 2026). Attention Residuals connect the depth, a Stable LatentMoE layer activates 16 of 896 experts under Quantile Balancing, and a Per-Head Muon optimizer runs a cosine schedule. DeepSeek-V4 trains 1.6 trillion parameters with 49 billion active on 33 trillion tokens (DeepSeek-AI, 2026). Its stack interleaves Compressed Sparse Attention with Heavily Compressed Attention, replaces plain residuals with Manifold-Constrained Hyper-Connections, keeps DeepSeekMoE, and runs Muon with hybrid Newton-Schulz iterations. GLM-5 trains 744 billion parameters with 40 billion active on 28.5 trillion tokens and introduces DeepSeek Sparse Attention through a 20-billion-token continued-pretraining adaptation of its dense base (GLM-5 Team, 2026). They agree as well on where four-bit precision enters. Kimi K3 applies MXFP4 weights and MXFP8 activations from supervised fine-tuning through reinforcement learning. DeepSeek-V4 applies FP4 quantization-aware training to expert weights and the indexer path during post-training.

Under independent scaling-law searches for each schedule, cosine decay reached a lower final loss than warmup-stable-decay (WSD) at every configuration tested, and Kimi K3 adopts cosine as its default (Kimi Team, 2026, Section 3.2). The per-token log-ratio reward matched top-k on-policy distillation objectives in convergence speed and final performance, and Kimi K3 keeps the simpler objective (Kimi Team, 2026, Section 4.1.3). Both results are Tier C and rest on one lab's runs. Each demotes an academic method from "absent" to "rejected," the fifth cell value the matrix records.

### 2.2 Post-training

The consolidated pipeline runs the same way at all three labs. Kimi K3 trains nine expert policies, three domains crossed with three reasoning-effort levels, then distills them into one student (Kimi Team, 2026). The per-token reward is the clipped log-ratio of teacher to student probability, and it rides on the same partial-rollout reinforcement-learning infrastructure as the expert stage. DeepSeek-V4 cultivates domain experts through supervised fine-tuning and GRPO and consolidates them through on-policy distillation (DeepSeek-AI, 2026). GLM-5.2 merges more than ten expert models through parallel on-policy distillation in about two days on the slime framework, and GLM-5.3 reuses the GLM-5.2 base and attributes every gain to further post-training on the same stack (Z.ai, 2026a; Z.ai, 2026b). Effort control enters as a reward term. Kimi K3 overrides the task reward with a penalty when output exceeds a per-problem budget scaled by a multiplier annealed per domain. GLM-5.2 and GLM-5.3 expose low, high, and max effort levels trained the same way.

Reward sources converge as well. Verifiable tasks use binary verifiers, and GLM-5.3 synthesizes verifiers without access to reference solutions and screens them with oracle, no-op, and unsolved-state checks. Open-ended tasks use an agentic generative reward model that reads the output, writes a rubric, scores each candidate against it, and records the scores (Kimi Team, 2026). A verbosity cap forfeits the comparison when a candidate exceeds a length multiple. GLM-5.2 adds an anti-hack module that filters tool calls by rule, judges intent with a language model, blocks the call, and returns dummy output so the rollout continues rather than collapsing the batch (Z.ai, 2026a). Long-horizon trajectories change the optimizer. GLM-5.2 replaces group-relative advantages with a critic-based proximal policy optimization on individual rollouts, so that compaction sub-traces of variable count and length all become trainable (Z.ai, 2026a). Kimi K3 tolerates extreme staleness from partial rollouts through a per-token regularizer that keeps updates in a local neighborhood (Kimi Team, 2026).

The converged labs name one open problem in their own words, and it concerns environments. The GLM-5.3 post states that the environment-synthesis pipelines "still require a meaningful amount of human-in-the-loop work" and that "making environment generation and verification more autonomous is one of the next steps" (Z.ai, 2026b), a statement Section 6 grades G3 beside the peer-reviewed self-play literature.

## 3. Path 1: from scratch

Kimi K2 and K3, DeepSeek-V4, and GLM-5 train with Muon (Kimi Team, 2025; Kimi Team, 2026; DeepSeek-AI, 2026; GLM-5 Team, 2026). Native Sparse Attention won the ACL 2025 best paper and became DeepSeek Sparse Attention (Yuan et al., 2025, ACL). Mixture of Block Attention serves Kimi's long context (Lu et al., 2025, NeurIPS). Gated attention won a NeurIPS 2025 best paper and ships in Qwen3-Next (Qiu et al., 2025a, NeurIPS). Global-batch load balancing ships in Qwen3's mixture of experts (Qiu et al., 2025b, ACL). Latent mixture of experts ships in Kimi K3 and Nemotron 3 (Elango et al., 2026, Tier C). The efficient-MoE scaling law that predicted a sevenfold leverage at 3 percent activation ships in Ling 2.0 (Tian et al., 2026, ICLR). Peer review reached the flagships within six to twelve months in each case. A second layer of peer-reviewed results, on hyperparameter transfer, schedules, checkpoint merging, data, objectives, memory, precision, and mid-training, sits outside every flagship. The sweep verified 82 sources on this path, one of them a labeled pre-window baseline: 39 at Tier A, 3 at Tier B, 17 at Tier C, and 22 at Tier D.

**Matrix-preconditioned optimizers keep their speedup only under hyperparameter transfer that the frontier does by heuristic (G4).** Muon, SOAP, and Shampoo hold a 1.4 times speedup over AdamW from 190 million to 1.4 billion parameters under maximal-update parameterization with weight decay scaled as one over width (Qiu et al., 2025c, NeurIPS). Under standard parameterization the speedup vanishes with scale. Matching Muon's update RMS to Adam's, the heuristic Moonlight introduced and Kimi K3 and DeepSeek-V4 inherit, is an incorrect width scaling under the same analysis. An independent benchmark at up to 8 times the Chinchilla token budget finds the matrix-preconditioned advantage decaying from 1.4 times at 100 million parameters to 1.1 times at 1.2 billion (Wen et al., 2026, ICLR). Its fitted law predicts Muon losing to AdamW at 7 billion parameters and one times Chinchilla. NVIDIA's frontier-scale study reports the opposite at 72 billion parameters, where Muon and SOAP keep token efficiency at 100-million-token batches that degrade AdamW, and names sharding of fine-grained MoEs as the scalability hurdle (Khona et al., 2026, Tier C). The flagships settle the question by running Muon at 0.7 to 2.8 trillion parameters. The Tier A prescription stays untested at that scale: a parameterization-correct transfer of learning rate and weight decay for a matrix-preconditioned optimizer at that scale, and a measurement of whether the 1.4 times survives there.

**Three peer-reviewed optimizer refinements sit below 6 billion parameters (G1).** Grouping attention heads six to a block beats both full-matrix Muon and the fully per-head split, and per-head wins early and loses later (Zhang et al., 2026, ICML). Kimi K3 uses Per-Head Muon and GLM-5 uses MuonSplit, both the per-head variant. Normalizing Muon's update per neuron improves training efficiency over Muon by 11 to 14 percent at 1.1 and 5.4 billion parameters (Li et al., 2026a, ICML). Optimal polynomial matrix-sign iterations converge about twice as fast as Newton-Schulz and lower validation loss across learning rates (Amsel et al., 2026, ICLR); DeepSeek-V4 uses a hybrid Newton-Schulz and Kimi K3 uses per-head Newton-Schulz. Late layers drop below the Newton-Schulz orthonormalization threshold at frontier scale unless they receive more iterations (Tier D spectral study, up to 2.8 billion parameters). The iteration count is therefore a layer-wise choice, and every flagship applies one count to all layers.

**Depth-wise hyperparameter transfer stops at 1.9 billion parameters (G1).** CompleteP transfers hyperparameters across depths from 2 to 128 layers and saves 12 to 34 percent of FLOPs over maximal-update parameterization, with the 34 percent at a 179-layer model (Dey et al., 2025, NeurIPS). Its largest test is 1.9 billion parameters, 1.5 billion of them non-embedding, and a state-of-the-art model trained with it is its authors' stated future work. Kimi K3 runs 93 layers and DeepSeek-V4-Pro runs 61, and both retune hyperparameters by in-house scaling-law sweeps (Kimi Team, 2026; DeepSeek-AI, 2026). Weight decay and batch size have their own Tier A power laws (Bergsma et al., 2025a, NeurIPS). The optimal decay follows a power of tokens per parameter, and the critical batch size scales as the square root of data, independent of model size. The flagships disclose a weight decay of 0.1 and a warmup of 1 percent. The laws that would set both are published and unapplied.

**The learning-rate schedule the frontier chose lost to a schedule it left untested (G2).** Kimi K3 compared cosine decay with warmup-stable-decay under independent scaling-law searches and adopted cosine, GLM-4.5 reached the same verdict, and Nemotron 3 trains under warmup-stable-decay (Kimi Team, 2026; GLM-4.5 Team, 2025, Tier C; NVIDIA, 2026a, Tier C). Linear decay to zero beats cosine, tenfold decay, and warmup-stable-decay, and the advantage grows with tokens per parameter (Bergsma et al., 2025b, ICLR). A 610-million-parameter model at 80 tokens per parameter with decay to zero matches 200 tokens per parameter with tenfold decay, about 60 percent of the compute. A multi-power law predicts loss under unseen schedules and yields an optimized schedule that beats cosine and grid-tuned warmup-stable-decay (Luo et al., 2025, ICLR). Both results are Tier A and stop below 2 billion parameters. Kimi K3's comparison is Tier C, covers two schedules, and stops where the third would begin.

**Checkpoint merging during pretraining has Tier A evidence at frontier-relevant scale, one disclosed adoption, and a named open problem at long decay horizons (G3).** Merging checkpoints from a constant-learning-rate run matches or beats an annealed model and predicts the annealed result (Li et al., 2025, NeurIPS). The evidence spans dense models from 411 million to 70 billion parameters and mixtures of experts up to 200 billion total parameters on 16 trillion tokens, and its authors report the method as ByteDance Seed's in-house practice. Nemotron 3 Super applies sliding-window checkpoint merging through its 25-trillion-token stable phase, gains 2 to 4 points over trained checkpoints across 12 benchmarks, saves about 16 percent of the pretraining FLOP budget in avoided decay runs, and selects a 500-billion-token merge as its final base (NVIDIA, 2026a, Tier C). The same report finds trained decay matching or surpassing merges at its 5-trillion-token decay horizon and states that merge schedules "tailored to longer decay horizons could recover the gains observed at shorter scales," which is the open problem. Kimi K3, DeepSeek-V4, and GLM-5 each describe a cooldown and leave merging unmentioned, so those three cells read "undisclosed" and carry the provisional label. The method is adopted at one open lab and in Ant Group's Ling line, and the flagship cells are the gap.

**Data-constrained recipes have an ICLR 2026 oral at 1.4 billion parameters, and the frontier is at 33 trillion tokens (G1).** Under fixed data, a joint recipe of regularization and ensembling reaches the asymptote of a 200-million-token corpus with one fifth of the data (Kim et al., 2026, ICLR). A distilled student keeps 83 percent of the ensemble gain, and the recipe gives 17.5 times data efficiency on mathematics mid-training. Diffusion language models tolerate about 500 epochs of repetition where autoregressive models tolerate 15 to 30, and the compute at which diffusion wins scales as the 2.2 power of unique tokens (Prabhudesai et al., 2025, NeurIPS). Synthetic rephrasing at trillion-token scale adds 2.6 points over the best prior synthetic corpus at 8 billion parameters and saturates in rephraser quality at 3 billion (Tier D). DeepSeek-V4 pretrains on 33 trillion tokens and GLM-5 on 28.5 trillion, so the flagships sit near the unique-token supply that the data-constrained literature estimates. Each leaves its epoch count, its ensemble use, and its repetition regime undisclosed.

**Alternative objectives have oral-track evidence below 10 billion parameters and one named compute ceiling (G1).** Energy-based transformers scale up to 35 percent faster than the strongest transformer baseline across data, batch, parameters, and depth, and gain 29 percent more from inference-time thinking, at up to 800 million parameters (Gladstone et al., 2026, ICLR). Joint-embedding prediction lifts pretraining accuracy from 54 to 61 percent on a synthetic corpus at about twice the compute (Huang et al., 2026, ICLR). The 8-billion-parameter diffusion model trained from scratch on 2.3 trillion tokens matches Llama-3 8B in in-context learning, and every comparison with autoregressive models stayed below 10 to the 23 FLOPs, in its authors' words (Nie et al., 2025, NeurIPS). Reinforcement as a pretraining objective adds 19 percent on eight benchmarks at 1.7 billion parameters and raises a 12-billion hybrid from 43 to 61 (Hatamizadeh et al., 2026, ICLR), and NVIDIA lists it in the Nemotron line. Gemini Diffusion and Mercury exist at undisclosed scale (Tier C and D). The frontier flagships are autoregressive next-token predictors with one multi-token head, and the 10-to-the-23 ceiling is the highest compute at which anyone has published a controlled alternative.

**Memory and state-space layers have Tier A results that their own labs' flagships omit (G2).** Conditional memory through scalable lookup adds 3.4 points of MMLU and 5 points of BBH at 27 billion parameters against a mixture of experts at equal parameters and FLOPs (Cheng et al., 2026, ACL). A 100-billion-parameter table sits on the host at under 3 percent overhead. The work is DeepSeek's, and DeepSeek-V4's architecture section describes compressed attention, hyper-connections, and Muon and omits any memory layer. Test-time memorization modules scale needle retrieval past 2 million tokens at 760 million parameters (Behrouz et al., 2025, NeurIPS). Mamba-3 improves on Gated DeltaNet by 0.6 to 1.8 points at 1.5 billion parameters and halves the state size (Lahoti et al., 2026, ICLR); Nemotron 3 uses Mamba-2 and Kimi K3 uses a delta-rule descendant. The frontier moved fastest on the residual stream: Attention Residuals at Moonshot and manifold-constrained hyper-connections at DeepSeek (Chen et al., 2026c, Tier C; Xie et al., 2025, Tier C), both Tier C at 27 to 48 billion parameters in their own ablations.

**Low-precision pretraining has two Tier A laws and one production run that disagree about the data limit (G3).** Floating-point quantization scaling laws identify a critical data size beyond which low-precision loss rises and place the best cost-performance precision at 4 to 8 bits (Sun et al., 2025, ICML). Native MXFP4 training is near-lossless in the large-data regime at up to 1.6 billion parameters with a 7-billion stability run (Castro et al., 2025, NeurIPS). Nemotron 3 Super is the first production model pretrained in NVFP4, on 25 trillion tokens at 120 billion parameters, and Nemotron 3 Ultra reports a pretraining loss gap under 0.4 percent to BF16 (NVIDIA, 2026a; NVIDIA, 2026b, Tier C). The Tier A law predicts a penalty at that token count. The Tier C runs report a gap under half a percent. The disagreement is measurable, and the measurement remains private.

**Mid-training and data-mixture laws are peer-reviewed at 7 billion parameters and disclosed in outline above it (G2).** A scaling law fitted at small scale predicts loss at 8 billion parameters and on unseen mixtures, and law-derived weights beat token-proportional and uniform mixtures downstream (Shukor et al., 2025, NeurIPS). Mid-training helps most for code and mathematics, and its timing interacts with its mixture: an early start tolerates 80 percent specialized data and a late start needs about 10 percent (Liu et al., 2026a, ICML). Mid-training restructures more than 90 percent of the weights while reinforcement learning changes about 5 percent, and reinforcement learning succeeds only after mid-training, across 3 to 24 billion parameters (Tier D). Every flagship describes a mid-training stage by its context lengths and token counts. Its mixture weights, its timing rule, and the law that set them stay in-house.

## 4. Path 2: from or through distilled models

On-policy distillation moved from a proposal to the consolidation stage of every disclosed frontier recipe within twelve months, and the peer-reviewed literature now documents nine properties of that method, from its missing scaling law to its ordering against reinforcement learning, that every frontier report leaves unmeasured. The sweep verified 88 sources on this path, two of them labeled baselines: 20 at Tier A, 1 at Tier B, 18 at Tier C, and 47 at Tier D, with 31 newer than May 2026. Kimi K3 discloses multi-teacher on-policy distillation from nine expert policies, and DeepSeek-V4 discloses on-policy distillation as its consolidation stage (Kimi Team, 2026; DeepSeek-AI, 2026). GLM-5.2 discloses a parallel on-policy distillation merge of more than ten experts (Z.ai, 2026a). Nemotron 3 Ultra discloses two iterations of multi-teacher distillation from more than ten domain teachers with a warm-up stage, and MiMo-V2-Flash and Nemotron-Cascade 2 disclose the same pattern at smaller scale (NVIDIA, 2026a; Xiaomi, 2026; NVIDIA, 2026b). Kimi K2.5 describes its supervised data as synthesized from earlier Kimi models and in-house experts and names no distillation stage, so on the second path "distilled model" describes Kimi K3 rather than its predecessor.

**Scaling laws for on-policy distillation are a named open problem (G3).** A distillation scaling law exists for offline distillation (Busbridge et al., 2025, ICML). It fits student cross-entropy as a function of student size, distillation tokens, and teacher cross-entropy, across students and teachers from 143 million to 12.6 billion parameters and up to 512 billion tokens. That law shows distillation beating supervised pretraining only below a student-size-dependent compute threshold and only when the teacher already exists. On-policy distillation lacks an equivalent law, and student rollouts add a compute axis to it. The unanswered question is whether more on-policy data can compensate for a weaker teacher or teacher quality sets a hard ceiling (Song & Zheng, 2026, survey, Tier D). Four labs run the stage, at costs from two days of frontier compute at Zhipu to two teacher-refresh iterations at NVIDIA, and each sizes it by convention.

**Cross-family teachers break the adopted method, and the peer-reviewed fix is untested above 8 billion parameters (G1).** Multi-teacher distillation from a Qwen3-235B teacher into a Qwen3-30B student diverges under the top-k objective and degrades under the policy-gradient objective, so same-origin teachers are required in practice (Ma et al., 2026, ICML workshop, Tier D). Every disclosed frontier consolidation stage distills within one model family, consistent with that result. Approximate likelihood matching over chunk alignments distills across different tokenizers, including subword to byte, at 7 to 8 billion parameters (Minixhofer et al., 2025, NeurIPS), and a 2026 extension carries it to the on-policy setting (Tier D). A frontier lab that wants a stronger external teacher, or a student with a new tokenizer, has a Tier A method, and the trial at scale awaits.

**Entropy-aware and self-distilled objectives carry ICML 2026 evidence at 4 to 8 billion parameters and sit outside every recipe (G1).** Entropy-aware on-policy distillation applies reverse KL on low-entropy teacher tokens and forward KL over the teacher's top-k where teacher entropy is high (Jin et al., 2026, ICML). It raises pass@8 by 1.4 to 5.1 points over standard on-policy distillation on students up to 4 billion parameters. On-policy self-distillation makes one model both teacher, conditioned on the reference solution, and student, and reaches four to eight times the token efficiency of GRPO on students up to 8 billion parameters (Zhao et al., 2026a, ICML). Privileged-information distillation trains the conditioned teacher and the unconditioned student with shared parameters and beats supervised-then-RL pipelines on agentic benchmarks at 7 billion parameters (Penaloza et al., 2026, ICML). Kimi K3 tested a top-k objective, a different variant, and found the sampled-token log-ratio reward matching it (Kimi Team, 2026). That single Tier C null result leaves the entropy-gated mixture and the privileged self-teacher untested at frontier scale. A Tier D study reports that privileged self-distillation degrades thinking models by up to 17 percent on competition mathematics, so the risk on the self-teacher branch is documented as well.

**On-policy distillation degrades along long trajectories, and frontier reports apply it to agentic tasks without a stated remedy (G3).** The teacher signal on the last 30 percent of rollout positions carries a small fraction of the learning, local teachability collapses under strong-to-weak distillation, and the dense token reward decays with trajectory depth (Tier D, three independent studies). "Systematic degradation of teacher supervision with trajectory depth" stands as a foundational open problem in the survey, which proposes hybrids of dense token supervision on short segments with sparse outcome rewards on long horizons (Song & Zheng, 2026, Tier D). Kimi K3 runs multi-teacher distillation on long-horizon agentic experts through partial rollouts and reports the consolidated result with no position-wise analysis. The peer-reviewed side holds the diagnosis at small scale. The frontier side holds the deployment at large scale. The remedy awaits both.

**Whether on-policy distillation expands capability at all is contested (G3).** Across teacher-student pairs up to 1024 samples per problem, on-policy distillation raises average accuracy and leaves the pass@1024 boundary at the pre-distillation base, while off-policy distillation expands that boundary (Tier D). Supervised fine-tuning memorizes and reinforcement learning generalizes out of distribution, with supervised data needed only to stabilize format before RL (Chu et al., 2025, ICML). Distillation raises pass@k only when the teacher supplies new knowledge, and pattern-only distillation behaves like RL with verifiable rewards (Tier D). The frontier consolidates capabilities that already exist in the experts, which is the case where the contested result is harmless. Using distillation to lift a student above its teachers' union is the case the evidence leaves open.

**Multi-teacher imbalance and teacher hacking have Tier A diagnoses and Tier D fixes (G1).** Teacher hacking is the state in which the student's distance to the teacher falls while its distance to the oracle rises (Tiapkin et al., 2025, ICML). It appears with fixed offline teacher data and disappears with as little as 10 percent online data or with prompt diversity. The frontier's on-policy setting therefore avoids it by construction, and the matrix records that cell as "hinted." The multi-teacher setting adds a second defect: standard multi-teacher distillation recovers 35.6 percent of the oracle ensemble headroom on a 3-billion-parameter student, and token-share balancing with gap-aware budgets raises that to 83.4 percent (Tier D, August 2026). Nemotron 3 Ultra inserts a warm-up supervised stage and two teacher co-evolution iterations to manage the same imbalance and leaves the iteration count unanalyzed (NVIDIA, 2026b). The fix is one month old, sits at 3 billion parameters, and addresses a stage that three frontier labs run.

**Distillation as a pretraining route stops at the small end of the frontier (G4).** Gemma 3, Apple's 2025 foundation models, and Llama 4's smaller members disclose distillation from a larger teacher during pretraining (Google, 2025; Apple, 2025; Meta, 2025). Kimi K3, DeepSeek-V4, and GLM-5 disclose supervised pretraining from scratch for their flagships. The distillation scaling law predicts that outcome below 12.6 billion parameters and stops there (Busbridge et al., 2025, ICML). Teacher-student compatibility rather than teacher strength governs pretraining distillation gains at up to 8 billion parameters (Tier D). Pretraining a frontier-scale student from a compatible frontier teacher below the supervised cost combines two adopted ingredients, distillation pretraining and trillion-parameter training, and the combination awaits its first report.

**Distilled bases as reinforcement-learning starting points carry conflicting Tier A and Tier C evidence (G3).** Distilling DeepSeek-R1's traces into small models beats running RL on those models directly (Guo et al., 2025, Nature). Pure RL on a 24-billion-parameter base matches distillation on competition mathematics, and RL on top of the distilled checkpoint scores highest (Mistral AI, 2025, Tier C). Both results sit below 30 billion parameters. The frontier labs run RL on natively pretrained bases and distill afterward, so the ordering question at frontier scale, distill then RL or RL then distill, remains a matter of lab convention rather than measured evidence.

## 5. Path 3: from leading open-weights models

The open-weights bases of 2026 are already mixture-of-experts models with sparse or hybrid attention, so the productive interventions on this path change what a released checkpoint can do at a fraction of its pretraining cost. The peer-reviewed record shows nine such interventions, validated between 2 billion and one trillion parameters, that every frontier recipe omits. The sweep verified all 95 sources on this path: 27 at Tier A, 9 at Tier B, 20 at Tier C, and 39 at Tier D. The frontier adopts four adaptations of a released base. DeepSeek-V3.2 converts a 685-billion-parameter dense-attention checkpoint to DeepSeek Sparse Attention through 943.7 billion tokens of continued pretraining, and GLM-5 reaches the same result on its own base in 1,000 warm-up steps and 20 billion tokens (DeepSeek-AI, 2025; GLM-5 Team, 2026). Llama-Nemotron prunes Llama 3.1-405B to 253 billion parameters, recovers with 153 billion tokens, and then exceeds the source through reinforcement learning (Bercovich et al., 2025, Tier C). Olmo 3, DAPO, and ProRL run reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards on open bases with full disclosure (Team OLMo, 2025, Tier C; Yu et al., 2025a, NeurIPS; Liu et al., 2025, NeurIPS). GLM-5.3 reuses the GLM-5.2 base and raises Terminal-Bench 3.0 from 4.6 to 28.3 through post-training alone (Z.ai, 2026b). Those four adaptations define "explored." The nine below define the gap.

**Conversion to linear or hybrid attention is validated at 72 billion parameters and rejected at one frontier lab on evidence the peer-reviewed fix addresses (G2).** A three-stage protocol of attention-weight transfer, hidden-state alignment, and distribution matching converts Qwen2.5 models at 7, 32, and 72 billion parameters to linear-attention decoders (Goldstein et al., 2025, COLM). The conversion uses 350 to 700 million tokens, under 0.005 percent of the teacher's pretraining tokens, and the quality stays close to the source. Post-training neural architecture search freezes the multilayer perceptrons of a pretrained model, learns where full attention must remain, and delivers a 2-billion-parameter hybrid that matches Qwen3-1.7B with 53.6 times the generation throughput at 256K context (Gu et al., 2025, NeurIPS). Gate construction from pretrained key projections recovers 93 percent of Llama-3-8B with 0.02 percent of its tokens (Lan et al., 2025, ICML). GLM-5 ran the conversion ablations, found a residual gap of 5.7 to 7.3 points for every sliding-window and gated-delta conversion it tried, and adopted sparse attention instead (GLM-5 Team, 2026). The residual gap has a Tier A diagnosis: which layers keep softmax attention governs the outcome, and scoring each layer by the KL reduction from restoring it beats fixed-ratio interleaving on long-context retrieval (Li et al., 2026c, ICLR). That selection method was tested at 3 billion parameters. The frontier ran the conversion at 744 billion parameters without it.

**Expert pruning is validated at one trillion parameters, and the frontier keeps the experts (G2).** Router-weighted expert saliency prunes 50 percent of the experts from Qwen3-Coder-480B and Kimi-K2 with at most a 2 percent drop on code generation (Lasby et al., 2026, ICLR). The same paper proves that expert merging causes functional subspace collapse on generative tasks. Every disclosed 2026 flagship ships its full expert set, and the one lab that prunes flagships, NVIDIA, prunes dense and hybrid Mamba models rather than experts (Bercovich et al., 2025; NVIDIA, 2025, Tier C). A frontier-class model at half the expert memory sits one peer-reviewed step from an existing checkpoint.

**Growth from a checkpoint has Tier A and Tier B recipes below 10 billion parameters and awaits a frontier trial (G1).** Drop-upcycling re-initializes part of each expert while upcycling a dense checkpoint and reaches 13-billion-dense quality with 5.9 billion active parameters at a quarter of the training compute (Nakamura et al., 2025, ICLR). Scaling laws for upcycling then identify an interaction term between the dense and upcycled datasets that limits efficiency at large budgets (Liew et al., 2025, ICML). Depth up-scaling with layers predicted from the singular-value structure of existing layers beats copy-based scaling at under half the continued-pretraining compute (Yang et al., 2025, ACL), and optimal-transport alignment of adjacent blocks improves progressive depth growth (Cao et al., 2025a, Tier B). Frontier bases are already MoE, so the relevant growth is expert expansion or depth growth of a released MoE, and the Tier D evidence for that case stops at 13 billion parameters. The interaction term is a named limit measured only at academic scale.

**Continued pretraining of a mixture of experts has one TMLR recipe and one named open problem (G3).** Across 600 billion tokens, MoE routers balanced by Sinkhorn or penalty terms tolerate distribution shift without replay, and replay with an infinite learning-rate schedule matches full retraining at a fraction of the cost (Therien et al., 2025, TMLR). Early MoE layers change the most during continued pretraining, and special treatment of those layers is the study's stated future work. Scaling laws for forgetting under pretraining-data injection make the replay ratio predictable (Bethune et al., 2025, Tier B), and self-generated samples serve as replay data that nearly eliminates forgetting (Tier D). GLM-5's 20-billion-token adaptation discloses the token count and the learning rate and leaves replay undisclosed, so the frontier cell reads "undisclosed" rather than "absent" and the gap is provisional. The layer-specific treatment is a G3 gap with a Tier A source.

**Merging reinforcement-learned experts is Tier A evidence for a step before distillation that every frontier recipe omits (G4).** Experts trained by reinforcement learning merge with less interference than experts trained by supervised fine-tuning, because their task vectors are closer to orthogonal (Ren et al., 2026, ICLR). Merging methods otherwise land between the base model and the individual fine-tunes, and sophisticated methods can lose to task arithmetic (He et al., 2025, Tier B). A controlled comparison at 30 billion parameters found multi-teacher on-policy distillation beating parameter merging by 5.5 normalized points (Ma et al., 2026, Tier D), and every disclosed frontier recipe consolidates by distillation rather than merging, consistent with that result. The untested combination is merging as a cheap initialization before distillation, which would start the student closer to the teachers' union at zero rollout cost. The combination awaits its first report, and merging "is essentially never tested against the baseline it claims to replace" in the reinforcement-learning setting (McClendon, 2026, Tier D).

**Quantization-aware training allocation has a Tier A law that overturns the frontier's fixed fraction (G3).** Across 757 runs up to 2.2 billion parameters, the optimal fraction of training spent under quantization grows with tokens per parameter-byte, and the common "10 percent" rule loses to a token-dependent fraction (Dremov et al., 2025, Tier B). Fusing the cooldown with quantization-aware training reaches the same loss at about half the compute at low bit widths. A second law over 268 runs finds W4A4 error falling with model size and rising with token count and group size (Chen et al., 2026a, ICML). Kimi K3 runs quantization-aware training from supervised fine-tuning onward, DeepSeek-V4 runs it in post-training, and Nemotron 3 Ultra pretrains in NVFP4 with under 0.4 percent loss gap to BF16 (Kimi Team, 2026; DeepSeek-AI, 2026; NVIDIA, 2026b). Each lab picked a fraction by convention. The law that would size it is extrapolated from 2.2 billion parameters, and the earlier assumptions "do not hold as compute budgets increase," in its authors' words.

**Long-context extension has Tier A evidence at 8 billion parameters and undisclosed recipes above it (G2).** Evolutionary rescaling of under-trained rotary dimensions with mixed-window training reaches 128K effective context on Llama-3-8B with 10 billion tokens, 80 times fewer than Meta's recipe (Shang et al., 2025, ICML). A continued-pretraining recipe with document separators reaches 4 million tokens on the same base (Xu et al., 2025, Tier C). Interleaving position-free global layers with windowed rotary layers converts an existing model at about 2 percent of its pretraining compute and generalizes beyond the training length (Puvvada et al., 2025, EMNLP). Kimi K3, DeepSeek-V4, and GLM-5.2 reach one million tokens through late-stage curricula whose rescaling method and token counts appear in outline only. The peer-reviewed extension methods stop at 8 billion parameters. The frontier methods stop at the outline.

**Reinforcement learning touches a small subnetwork, and frontier runs update the whole model (G4).** Across seven algorithms and ten models, reinforcement learning updates 5 to 30 percent of parameters, and the subnetworks overlap across seeds and algorithms (Mukherjee et al., 2025, NeurIPS). Low-rank adaptation matches full fine-tuning for reinforcement learning even at low rank (Schulman et al., 2025, Tier D). Every frontier post-training run disclosed in the window is full-parameter, and GLM-5.3 spends a month of post-training compute on a 744-billion-parameter base. Restricting frontier reinforcement learning to the subnetwork it would touch anyway combines two documented facts, and the combination awaits its first test.

A pretrained plug-and-play memory decoder that imitates a nearest-neighbor retriever lowers perplexity by 6.2 points across biomedicine, finance, and law with the base weights untouched (Cao et al., 2025b, NeurIPS), and every frontier base ships without one. Post-hoc multi-token-prediction heads on a frozen backbone reach nearly 5 times faster code generation only with gated low-rank adapters, because ungated adapters degrade the next-token head (Tier D). The frontier keeps its heads from pretraining, so open bases without a head, the Llama line included, have a route that awaits its first report.

Olmo 3 names the open problem that frames the whole path. Every open benchmark and algorithm for reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) trains on bases "that do not reveal their pretraining or mid-training data," so the interaction between mid-training data and reinforcement learning stays unstudied (Team OLMo, 2025, Tier C). Mid-training content bounds the reinforcement-learning gain (Tier D), and across more than 100 models continued pretraining enables the downstream reinforcement-learning gain (Qi et al., 2025, NeurIPS). GLM-5.3 lifts Terminal-Bench 3.0 from 4.6 to 28.3 on a fixed base through post-training alone (Z.ai, 2026b). The evidence that would say how far, and why, requires the disclosure that only Olmo and Nemotron provide.

## 6. Path 4: from existing frontier models through post-training

The frontier discloses more of its post-training than of any other stage, and the peer-reviewed record answers three of the questions those disclosures leave open: what raises the reinforcement-learning ceiling rather than its efficiency, which tokens carry the learning signal, and how a model can build its own training environments. The sweep verified all 89 sources on this path against a primary page: 30 at Tier A, 9 at Tier B, 25 at Tier C, and 25 at Tier D. DeepSeek-R1's pure reinforcement learning on a 671-billion-parameter base is the first frontier recipe to pass peer review (Guo et al., 2025, Nature). DAPO's clip-higher, dynamic sampling, and token-level loss reach 50 on AIME 2024 from a 32-billion base and appear in GLM-5 under an upper clip of 0.28 (Yu et al., 2025a, NeurIPS; GLM-5 Team, 2026). Fully asynchronous training with a staleness cap and a decoupled objective gives a 2.8 times speedup at matched accuracy, and Ant Group's trillion-parameter Ring models report using it (Fu et al., 2025, NeurIPS; Ling Team, 2025, Tier C). Rubrics generated per instance beat Likert judges by up to 31 percent on HealthBench and appear in Kimi K3's agentic reward model, which writes a rubric before it scores (Gunjal et al., 2026, ICLR; Kimi Team, 2026). On-policy data explains why reinforcement learning forgets less than supervised fine-tuning, and that finding is the stated rationale for the on-policy consolidation stage at three labs (Shenfeld et al., 2026, ICLR; Chen et al., 2026b, ICML).

**The interventions the frontier tunes change reinforcement-learning efficiency, and the two that raise the ceiling cost the most to tune (G3).** A 400,000-GPU-hour study fits sigmoid compute-performance curves to reinforcement-learning runs at up to a 17-billion-by-16-expert mixture (Khatri et al., 2026, ICLR). Loss aggregation, curriculum, length penalty, and advantage normalization move the efficiency term and leave the asymptote fixed. Only context length and model size move the asymptote. Kimi K3 reports that tool-call steps and capability scale together with reinforcement-learning FLOPs, and GLM-5.3 reports a month of further post-training on a fixed base moving Terminal-Bench 3.0 from 4.6 to 28.3 (Kimi Team, 2026; Z.ai, 2026b). Both reports leave the asymptote unfitted. The knob that the frontier's month of compute moved remains unmeasured, and the knobs beyond those two that raise the ceiling at 744 billion to 2.8 trillion parameters remain untested.

**Whether reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards expands capability is contested at Tier A and unmeasured at frontier scale (G3).** Base models surpass their reinforcement-learned versions at 256 samples per problem across six algorithms and two families, and distillation expands the solution boundary where reinforcement learning sharpens it (Yue et al., 2025, NeurIPS oral). Prolonged reinforcement learning past 2,000 steps with reference resets finds strategies absent from the base's samples even under extensive sampling, at 1.5 billion parameters (Liu et al., 2025, NeurIPS). Counting correct intermediate reasoning rather than final answers restores a reinforcement-learning gain hidden by answer-only pass@k (Wen et al., 2026, ICLR). Support shrinkage exceeds expansion by about 3.6 to 1 for the prolonged-training checkpoints as well, and the remedy named is to seed probability mass into underrepresented solution regions (Wu et al., 2025, Tier B). Qwen3 measured a pass@64 gain of zero from reinforcement learning on an 8-billion student and chose distillation at one tenth the GPU hours (Qwen Team, 2025, Tier C). Every flagship leaves its large-k pass@k boundary unpublished. The debate has three Tier A entries and one Tier B entry, and the frontier's evidence is a single Tier C null result at 8 billion parameters.

**A minority of tokens carries the reinforcement-learning signal, and the frontier updates all of them (G2).** Restricting policy-gradient updates to the 20 percent of tokens with the highest entropy adds 11 points on AIME 2025 for a 32-billion base, with a gain that grows with scale (Wang et al., 2025, NeurIPS). Entropy collapse follows a law in which the change in entropy tracks the covariance of log-probability and advantage (Cui et al., 2025a, Tier B). Clipping or penalizing the high-covariance tokens adds 6.4 points over GRPO at 32 billion parameters, where entropy bonuses and reference KL lose ground. Preserving low-probability exploratory tokens sustains exploration past the collapse plateau (Huang et al., 2026b, ACL Findings). A pass@k reward with an entropy-guided switch raises pass@k without losing pass@1 on 7- and 32-billion dense and MoE models (Chen et al., 2025, Tier B). Under 4 percent of tokens recover the full reinforcement-learning gain on a 7-billion model (Tier D). Kimi K3 constrains every token through a per-token regularizer and GLM-5.2 applies a token-level loss to compaction sub-traces (Kimi Team, 2026; Z.ai, 2026a). The Tier A evidence at 32 billion parameters says the signal is sparse. The frontier objective treats it as dense.

**Self-play and environment construction are the frontier's own named open problem, with Tier A methods at 4 to 14 billion parameters (G3).** GLM-5.3 synthesizes environments and verifiers through research agents and a judge agent, and the pipelines "still require a meaningful amount of human-in-the-loop work" while autonomous environment generation "is one of the next steps," in the post's own words (Z.ai, 2026b). A single model that proposes and solves abduction, deduction, and induction tasks with a learnability reward reaches state of the art among zero-data models at 14 billion parameters (Zhao et al., 2025, NeurIPS). Self-play on zero-sum text games with role-conditioned advantages adds 10 percent across eight reasoning benchmarks at 8 billion parameters and beats supervised fine-tuning on 25,000 expert trajectories (Liu et al., 2026b, ICLR). A challenger-solver pair improves a 4-billion base by 6.5 points on mathematics and collapses after about three iterations (Huang et al., 2026c, ICLR). Self-play in adaptive synthetic executable environments adds 5.3 points on eight held-out benchmarks at 30 billion parameters (Tier D, August 2026). The frontier has the need, in its own words, and the peer-reviewed side has methods that collapse after three iterations at 4 billion parameters. Each side awaits the other's trial.

**Learned effort control is peer-reviewed at 1.5 to 7 billion parameters, and the frontier trains one expert per effort level (G2).** Length-controlled policy optimization lets a prompt specify the target length and beats budget forcing by more than 20 points at 1.5 billion parameters (Aggarwal & Welleck, 2025, COLM). A constrained objective that prefers no-thinking subject to accuracy cuts length by 53 percent and adds 2.4 points (Zhang et al., 2025, EMNLP). A control token with a decoupled objective removes 50 to 90 percent of long-form reasoning on easy sets (Fang et al., 2025, NeurIPS). Kimi K3 trains nine experts, three domains by three effort levels, under a budget multiplier annealed per domain "under human-in-the-loop guidance," and distills them into one model; DeepSeek-V4 and GLM-5.3 expose fixed effort modes (Kimi Team, 2026; DeepSeek-AI, 2026; Z.ai, 2026b). A single run that learns effort control end to end is the Tier A method. Nine runs and a distillation is the frontier method.

**Dense and verifier-free rewards have Tier B evidence and one named gap, verifiable partial credit (G3).** An implicit process reward model defined as a log-ratio and updated online from outcome labels alone gives 2.5 times sample efficiency at 7 billion parameters (Cui et al., 2025b, Tier B). Self-certainty replaces the verifier and matches GRPO on mathematics with a 65 percent relative transfer gain to code (Zhao et al., 2026b, ICLR). The mean token probability of a reference answer extends verifiable-reward training to general domains (Yu et al., 2025b, Tier B). Entropy minimization alone matches GRPO on Qwen and gives no gain on Llama (Agarwal et al., 2025, NeurIPS). Random and incorrect rewards add 21 points on Qwen2.5-Math through clipping bias and add zero points on other families (Shao et al., 2026, ICML), so every small-model reward result needs a family control. "Verifiable partial credit" without the bias of learned critics is the open gap for practitioners (Srivastava & Aggarwal, 2026, ACM TIST). Kimi K3 uses binary verifiers and a rubric-writing judge, and GLM-5.3 synthesizes binary verifiers. Every lab scores in binary.

**Multi-turn credit assignment has a Tier A method at 7 billion parameters, and the frontier approximates it with a critic (G2).** Combining episode-level advantages with anchor-state step-level advantages, critic-free, adds 12 percent on ALFWorld and 9 percent on WebShop over GRPO at the same cost (Feng et al., 2025, NeurIPS). GLM-5.2 moved from group-relative advantages to a critic-based proximal policy optimization on individual rollouts so that compaction sub-traces of variable count and length become trainable (Z.ai, 2026a). The compaction method's own paper calls cross-trajectory GAE "an approximation" (Li et al., 2026b, Tier C). Turn-level critics beat token-level critics on multi-turn tasks in Tier D studies. The frontier's long-horizon objective is a Tier C approximation, and the Tier A alternative stops at 7 billion parameters.

**Forgetting and the sparsity of reinforcement-learning updates are measured at 3 to 8 billion parameters and assumed above (G3).** Forgetting is predicted by the KL divergence from the base on the new task, and on-policy reinforcement learning is implicitly KL-minimal (Shenfeld et al., 2026, ICLR). Its behavior "at frontier scales and in more diverse generative domains remains unknown," in the authors' words. Reinforcement-learned task vectors are sparse, off-principal, and near-orthogonal across runs, so task arithmetic and its successors degrade merged models and a Fisher-arbitrated merge recovers them (Wu et al., 2026, KDD; Yuan et al., 2026, ACL). The frontier answers forgetting with on-policy distillation alone, which Path 3 records as the G4 merge-then-distill gap, and that answer rests on a 3-billion-parameter measurement and a rationale.

**Test-time reinforcement learning is a NeurIPS 2025 method for a paradigm DeepSeek-V4 names as its next (G1).** Majority-vote pseudo-labels on unlabeled test prompts serve as a rule-based reward and lift pass@1 on AIME 2024 by 211 percent for a 7-billion model (Zuo et al., 2025, NeurIPS). Million-token context "establishes a necessary foundation for exploring future paradigms like online learning," in DeepSeek-V4's words (DeepSeek-AI, 2026). Every flagship freezes at deployment.

Placing a chain-of-thought monitor inside the reward produces obfuscated reward hacking and drops monitor recall toward zero, so OpenAI proposes a "monitorability tax" of withholding optimization pressure from the chain of thought (Baker et al., 2025, Tier C). Reinforcement learning on a mixture of experts diverges without routing replay under GRPO, and non-deterministic top-k in a sparse-attention indexer collapsed GLM-5's reinforcement learning within steps until a deterministic kernel replaced it (Zheng et al., 2025, Tier C; GLM-5 Team, 2026). The train-inference mismatch behind that collapse has Tier B evidence and a Tier D claim that it is a first-order factor (Yao et al., 2025, Tier B). The frontier closed it by engineering. Kimi K3 runs rollout and training under the same quantization, DeepSeek-V4 ships batch-invariant kernels, and GLM-5.3 aligns training and rollout numerics to a log-probability difference of 10 to the minus 7 (Kimi Team, 2026; DeepSeek-AI, 2026; Z.ai, 2026b). That is one gap the frontier closed ahead of peer review.

## 8. Threats to the findings

Six limits bound the register. Non-disclosure is the largest. Absence in a report means the report omits the method, and a lab that runs checkpoint merging or entropy-selective updates in-house without saying so would turn a G2 into an adoption. The matrix separates "absent," where the report describes the stage and omits the method, from "undisclosed," where the report leaves the stage unaddressed, and ranks 14 and 16 rest on undisclosed cells and carry the provisional label. Disclosure asymmetry is the second limit. The six matrix columns belong to Chinese labs and NVIDIA because those labs disclose recipes, and the proprietary system cards from OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google disclose evaluations rather than algorithms, so every finding about "the frontier" is a finding about its disclosing half. Family effects are the third. Random rewards improve Qwen2.5-Math through clipping bias and leave other families unchanged (Shao et al., 2026, ICML), and entropy-minimization gains appear on Qwen and vanish on Llama (Agarwal et al., 2025, NeurIPS). Every Tier A result below 10 billion parameters that used one family therefore carries an unmeasured family term. Recency is the fourth. The reference list holds 20 Tier B items after deduplication across paths, NeurIPS 2026 decisions among them on September 24, 2026, and the ranking would change if the entropy-mechanism, pass@k, and implicit-process-reward submissions publish. Channel bias is the fifth. Exa and web search rank by traffic. The sweeps corrected for it with venue filters and reference-list snowballing from six seed reports, and a method with Tier A evidence and no citation from a frontier report could still be missing. Single-session execution is the sixth. The sweep ran on one date with one set of tools, and the search log records every query so that a later run can measure drift.

## 9. Conclusion

The frontier converged in 2026 on one training recipe per path, and the unexplored methods are the ones that carry peer-reviewed evidence at academic scale and sit outside that recipe. On the pretraining path, the converged recipe runs a Muon-family optimizer, an attention stack that is sparse or partly linear, a residual-stream upgrade, and an ultra-sparse mixture of experts, and it leaves parameterization-correct transfer, schedule choice, and memory layers on the peer-reviewed side of the line. On the distillation path, the recipe consolidates experts by on-policy distillation and leaves its scaling law, its cross-family form, and its long-horizon decay unmeasured. On the open-weights path, the recipe converts attention and prunes dense models and leaves layer-selected conversion, expert pruning, and merge-then-distill untried at the scale where peer review already tested them. On the post-training path, the recipe scales reinforcement-learning compute and synthesizes environments by hand, and it leaves the asymptote, the sparse token signal, and autonomous environment generation to the literature that has already named them. Convergence of that breadth made absence measurable. The rival reading, novelty by recency, would have surfaced the newest on-policy distillation variants and missed the NeurIPS 2025 result that ranks first, a hyperparameter-transfer prescription that every flagship replaces with a heuristic, and the ICLR 2026 result that ranks third, an expert-pruning method validated on a trillion-parameter checkpoint that every flagship ships unpruned. Two registers, one matrix, four grades, and one ranking found them.
